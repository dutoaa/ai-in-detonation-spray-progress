Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethyleneComputational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Robust Monitoring of Arc Welding Processes: A Generalizable Framework with DVAE and Particle FilterRobust Monitoring of Arc Welding Processes: A Generalizable Framework with DVAE and Particle Filter

Yue Cao, Hai Lin, YuMing Zhang

Arc welding processes are essential for continuous fabrication but prone to disturbances that impair weld quality, making real-time monitoring critical yet difficult due to complex visual patterns and nonlinear, time-varying dynamics. Deep learning shows promise but faces scalability limits because of its dependence on large labeled datasets and application-specific tuning. We explore whether a unified approach can characterize major arc welding processes across applications and improve scalability through consistent state monitoring. This paper introduces a robust and generalizable monitoring framework for arc welding. It combines unsupervised deep latent representation learning, which extracts compact features from weld pool images, with Bayesian filtering to handle persistent and fluctuating disturbances such as arc radiation and specular reflections. Specifically, a Dynamic Variational Autoencoder (DVAE), consisting of a CNN-based encoder-decoder and an LSTM-based transition model, jointly learns latent representations and their evolution under control inputs. For robust real-time inference, a specialized Particle Filter (PF) propagates the latent and LSTM hidden states, preserving process history while suppressing sensor noise. This design is well suited to welding's slow and inertial dynamics. This study presents a real-time machine vision system that has been implemented on an automotive cabin assembly line to detect weld defects in door frames. Effect of Barrel Filling Ratio on the Microstructure, Phase Composition and Tribological Performance of Detonation-Sprayed Cr3C2–NiCr CoatingsEffect of Barrel Filling Ratio on the Microstructure, Phase Composition and Tribological Performance of Detonation-Sprayed Cr3C2–NiCr Coatings

Zhuldyz Sagdoldina, Aiym Nabioldina, Daryn Baizhan, Nurbol Berdimuratov, Gulsym Bektasova

This study investigates the influence of barrel filling ratio on the microstructure, phase composition, and tribological performance of detonation-sprayed Cr3C2–NiCr coatings. Coatings were deposited at barrel filling ratios of 43% and 53% under identical spraying conditions. Microstructural characterization revealed the formation of dense lamellar coatings with low porosity and uniform distribution of Cr3C2 carbide particles within the NiCr metallic matrix. Compared with the coating deposited at a barrel filling ratio of 43%, the coating deposited at 53% exhibited a denser microstructure. X-ray diffraction analysis confirmed that Cr3C2 and NiCr remained the dominant phases after spraying, while a minor amount of Cr7C3 formed due to partial decarburization of chromium carbide during thermal exposure. Tribological performance was evaluated under dry sliding conditions using a ball-on-disc configuration at normal loads of 10 and 15 N and sliding speeds of 5 and 10 cm/s. Wear volume was determined from the geometry of the wear track after testing, and wear rate was calculated accordingly. The coating produced at a barrel filling ratio of 53% demonstrated improved wear resistance under elevated loads despite exhibiting a higher coefficient of friction.
