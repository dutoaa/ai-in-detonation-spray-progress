Post-processing strategies for generative design outcomes in a UAV tail boom无人机尾撑生成式设计结果的后处理策略

Michał Piotr Kowalik, Piotr Stapinski, Mateusz Papis

Abstract Generative design produces structurally efficient but organically shaped geometries that require post-processing before Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) manufacturing. This paper compares two post-processing strategies applied to the same generative design outcome for an Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) tail boom: a complex variant faithful to the raw topology, and a simplified variant with symmetric arms, self-supporting geometry, and uniform cross-sections oriented toward FDM manufacturability. Both variants were evaluated using linear static and buckling finite element analysis underidentical loading conditions. Scaled prototypes of the simplified variant were FDM-printed at two infill configurations and physically load-tested. The manufacturing-oriented simplification reduced both mass and print time while eliminating support structures; peak stress remained well below the material limit. FEA-predicted stress concentrations correlated with observed fracture locations in physical tests. A study on porosity formation in cold-spray additively manufactured 6061 aluminium parts using MIG, TIG and FSW welding processes采用MIG、TIG和FSW焊接工艺的冷喷涂增材制造6061铝零件气孔形成研究

Aravinthan Arumugam, Ryan Riedel, Krishnan Kannoorpatti

Abstract Cold Spray Additive Manufacturing (CSAM) is a solid-state additive manufacturing process that enables components to be produced and repaired without melting the deposited material. However, limited research has examined the weldability of CSAM components. This study investigates porosity formation and local hardness responses in CSAM 6061 aluminium joined using Tungsten Inert Gas (TIG), Metal Inert Gas (MIG), and Friction Stir Welding (FSW), considering both non-heat-treated (NHT) and heat-treated (HT) conditions. Macroscopic, micrographic, ImageJ-based porosity analysis and Vickers hardness measurements were used to characterise the welded regions. TIG and MIG produced substantial fusion zone porosity with characteristics consistent with hydrogen-assisted pore formation during melting and solidification. Pre-weld heat treatment reduced the measured two-dimensional pore area fraction from 26.3% to 18.6% for TIG and from 30.7% to 22.1% for MIG, while also altering pore size and spatial distribution. TIG exhibited relatively large, interconnected pores extending from the fusion boundary, whereas MIG showed a greater population of smaller pores concentrated towards the upper weld region. Both fusion processes produced local hardness reductions, with hardness recovery of approximately 50–56% for TIG and 63–67% for MIG relative to the corresponding control regions. In contrast, FSW exhibited substantially less macro-porosity, predominantly finer residual microporosity, and hardness recovery of approximately 82–84%. Honghua Han, Xu Yan, Yanhua Zhao, Suhong Zhang, Qingyu Shi, Chengle Yang

Abstract The incorporation of dual shoulders endows bobbin tool friction stir welding (BT-FSW) with spatially distinct heat transfer and material flow characteristics relative to conventional FSW. The vague understanding of these processes hinders efficient optimization of BT-FSW. To address this gap, a computational fluid dynamics (CFD) model, which considered the fully coupling of thermo-mechanical-fluid, was established. Three contact pressure models, namely the Front-Peaked Contact Pressure Model (FP-CPM), Uniform Contact Pressure Model (U-CPM), and Rear-Peaked Contact Pressure Model (RP-CPM), were proposed to model the pressure distribution at the tool-workpiece (T/W) interface. The results show that the distribution of contact pressure at the T/W interface constitutes a critical factor governing the temperature field and flow behavior during welding stage. The FP-CPM provided superior predictive accuracy for the welding temperature, the shape of plastic deformation zone, and material flow trajectories, compared to U-CPM and RP-CPM. Agreed by both experiment and prediction, the temperature on the retreating side (RS) exceeded that on the advancing side (AS). Revealed by FP-CPM model, this phenomenon is attributable to the deflection of high-temperature material from the AS toward the RS, driven by the intense material flow ahead of the tool induced by the high-pressure zone. At the T/W interface, the material exhibited asymmetric horizontal flow with a thin layer ahead of the tool and a broader low-speed region behind the tool. Concurrently, vertical migration featured circumferential flow around the tool converging toward the plate mid-thickness.摘要 双轴肩的引入使双轴肩搅拌摩擦焊（BT-FSW）具有不同于传统搅拌摩擦焊（FSW）的空间传热与材料流动特征。对这些过程的认识尚不清晰，阻碍了BT-FSW的高效优化。为弥补这一不足，建立了考虑热-力-流完全耦合的计算流体力学（CFD）模型。提出了三种接触压力模型，即前部峰值接触压力模型（FP-CPM）、均匀接触压力模型（U-CPM）和后部峰值接触压力模型（RP-CPM），用于模拟工具-工件（T/W）界面的压力分布。结果表明，T/W界面的接触压力分布是控制焊接阶段温度场和流动行为的关键因素。与U-CPM和RP-CPM相比，FP-CPM对焊接温度、塑性变形区形状和材料流动轨迹的预测精度更高。实验与预测结果均表明，后退侧（RS）的温度高于前进侧（AS）。FP-CPM模型揭示，这一现象源于高压区在工具前方诱发的强烈材料流动，驱使高温材料从AS偏转至RS。在T/W界面，材料呈现不对称的水平流动特征，工具前方的流动层较薄，而工具后方存在较宽的低速区。同时，竖直方向的迁移表现为绕工具的周向流动向板厚中部汇聚。

Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution.
