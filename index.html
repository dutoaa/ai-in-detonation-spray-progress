Characteristic defect detection in laser micro-welding using distinct optical signal signatures and machine learning基于独特光学信号特征和机器学习的激光微焊中特征缺陷检测

Evangelos Skoulas, Nicola Nesa, Steven Ammann, Fabian Maag, Elia Iseli, Sergey A. Shevchik

The term ‘defect’ is widely used in the fields of laser welding and process monitoring for a broad range of deviations from what is considered a standard or good weld. However, whether a given anomalous weld is to be considered critically defective often depends on the intended application and on the specific kind of its abnormality. In this work we present a detailed investigation on characteristic defect recognition and classification during high-speed laser micro-welding of thin stainless-steel sheets with varying laser incidence angle. The study focuses on the correlation between optical emission signals, defect type, and physical origin under dynamic welding conditions. A pure optical sensing system was assembled and deployed to simultaneously monitor the laser reflection, near-infrared thermal emission and visible emission during the process. Controlled variations in process parameters such as welding power, focus variation, and inter-sheet gap were used to intentionally generate typical defects, including lack of weld penetration, hole, thickening formation and transitions between keyhole and conduction welding regimes. The relation between signal patterns, welding defects, and their physical origin was studied. The results reveal distinct and reproducible signal signatures associated with each of these process states; the keyhole-to-conduction transition is characterized qualitatively. Further machine learning experiments using a simple algorithm to classify chunks of the optical signal demonstrate that our easily deployable setup can exploit optical signals to recognize specific defects. The classifier is trained on four classes: good weld, lack of weld, hole and thickening. For the off-axis welds of the present dataset, recall values exceed 90% for all but one of the four classes. Finally, a single-run, exploratory case study suggests that knowledge can be partially transferable from intentionally generated defects to real, spontaneous defects. The proposed method therefore provides a practical basis for application-specific online defect assessment in laser micro-welding.“缺陷”一词在激光焊接和过程监测领域被广泛使用，用于描述偏离标准或良好焊缝的各种异常。然而，某一异常焊缝是否应被视为严重缺陷，往往取决于其预期应用以及异常的具体类型。本文对在不同激光入射角条件下薄不锈钢板高速激光微焊过程中的特征缺陷识别与分类进行了详细研究。该研究重点关注动态焊接条件下光学发射信号、缺陷类型与物理成因之间的相关性。我们搭建并部署了一套纯光学传感系统，在过程中同时监测激光反射、近红外热发射和可见发射。通过有控制地改变焊接功率、焦点偏移和板间间隙等工艺参数，刻意生成典型缺陷，包括未焊透、孔洞、增厚形成以及小孔焊与导热焊工艺状态之间的转变。本文研究了信号模式、焊接缺陷及其物理成因之间的关系。结果表明，各工艺状态对应着彼此不同且可重复的信号特征；并对小孔焊向导热焊的转变进行了定性表征。进一步采用简单算法对光学信号片段进行分类的机器学习实验表明，我们这一易于部署的系统能够利用光学信号识别特定缺陷。分类器在四类样本上进行训练：良好焊缝、未焊接、孔洞和增厚。对于本数据集中的离轴焊缝，除四类中的一类外，其余各类的召回率均超过90%。最后，一项单次、探索性的案例研究表明，部分知识可以从人为生成的缺陷迁移到真实的自发缺陷。因此，所提出的方法为激光微焊中面向具体应用的在线缺陷评估提供了实用基础。

Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量预测的计算与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. This study presents an effective Machine Learning (ML)-based framework for predicting the mechanical properties of Friction Stir Welded (FSW) joints using key welding parameters. Tool Rotational Speed (TRS), Traverse Speed (TTS), and Tool Plunge Depth (TPD) were considered as input variables, while Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS) and micro-hardness were selected as output responses. Experimental data were used to develop and evaluate five regression models: Linear Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Regression (SVR), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN). Model performance was assessed using Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Relative Absolute Error (RAE), and Root Relative Squared Error (RRSE) with an 80:20 training–testing split. Among the developed models, ensemble learning techniques demonstrated superior predictive capability for UTS. The Random Forest model achieved the best performance with the lowest testing MAE and RMSE values of 9.007 MPa and 12.975 MPa, respectively, while XGBoost also showed strong prediction accuracy. For micro-hardness prediction, SVR with a radial basis function kernel produced the highest accuracy, with MAE and RMSE values of 0.923 HV and 1.155 HV, respectively. Experimental validation confirmed that optimized welding conditions of 900 rpm rotational speed, 20 mm/min traverse speed, and 4.89 mm plunge depth significantly enhanced weld performance, resulting in a UTS of 281 MPa, micro-hardness of 138.4 HV, and refined nugget zone grain size of approximately 7 μm.
