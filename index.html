Deep Learning-Based Welding Process Optimization and Adaptive Control Method for Welding Robots基于深度学习的焊接机器人焊接工艺优化与自适应控制方法

Hai-Tao Li, Tian Xiao-long, Peng Zhang, Feng Yang, Tao Wang, Ci-En Li

GMAW (gas metal arc welding) robots are frequently used in contemporary manufacturing as they offer high levels of productivity, repeatable process stability and less need for manual labour. However, even today practical robotic welding is often based upon manual design, teaching and offline programming. Welding parameters and trajectories must be redesigned whenever the workpiece, joint geometries or assembly conditions change. Furthermore, during welding, changes to the weld-seam state caused by machining error, assembly deviation and cyclic heat input result in weld parameters planned for ideal conditions becoming inadequate and the risk of incomplete fusion, porosity, cracking, slag inclusions and unstable backside formations greatly increased. In this paper a method based on deep learning is presented to provide optimization of the welding process and adaptive control for GMAW robots. This method consists of integrated quality grade oriented process planning; site-specifically improved multi-objective parameter optimization; semi-supervised semantic segmentation for the perception of weld seams and molten pools and their associated spatter suppression through neural networks predicting weld formation; and fuzzy adaptive control logic. Rotational speed, welding speed, and shoulder plunge depth were used as input variables, with UTS as the output. Machine learning models were developed using 27 experimental samples and evaluated by leave-one-out cross-validation. Comparison of decision tree, K-nearest neighbors, and Gaussian process regression identifies decision tree as the best baseline model, with an R 2 of 0.905. SHapley Additive exPlanations analysis shows that the importance of the welding parameters follows the order of welding speed, rotational speed, and shoulder plunge depth, and the identified parameter effects agree well with the experimental results and observed microstructural evolution. The optimized model predicts fully feasible ranges of all the welding parameters for a joint efficiency of at least 90%, and the shoulder plunge depth acts as a key parameter for further refining the high-strength welding process window. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Two protective coatings, 80Ni20Cr and NiCrAlY, were deposited on Al 6061-T6 alloy substrates using the Detonation Gun (D-Gun) thermal spray process. The deposited coatings were studied for their microstructure, phase composition, surface roughness, hardness, corrosion behaviour, wettability, and solid particle erosion resistance. Characterisation was done using SEM-EDS, XRD, AFM, nanoindentation, potentiodynamic polarisation, contact angle measurement, and erosion testing. SEM-EDS results showed that both coatings bonded well to the substrate with uniform thickness. The NiCrAlY coating had a denser microstructure with lower porosity compared to 80Ni20Cr. XRD identified CrNi phases with FCC crystal structure in 80Ni20Cr and Al₂NiY phases with orthorhombic structure in NiCrAlY. Nanocrystalline grain structure was observed in both coatings. AFM measurements showed that NiCrAlY had the lowest surface roughness among all three samples. Nanoindentation results showed that NiCrAlY hardness is approximately 10 times higher than 80Ni20Cr. Specifically, NiCrAlY showed a hardness of 7895.36 MPa (731.2 HV), compared with 780.11 MPa (72.25 HV) for 80Ni20Cr and 708.68 MPa for the uncoated Al 6061 substrate. Potentiodynamic polarisation tests in 3.5 wt% NaCl solution confirmed that NiCrAlY recorded the lowest corrosion current density of 6.625 × 10 -7 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.00567 mmpy. The 80Ni20Cr coating gave a corrosion current density of 1.585 × 10 - 6 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.01427 mmpy. Both figures are considerably lower than the bare substrate at 0.5185 mmpy. Contact angle measurements confirmed superhydrophobic behaviour for NiCrAlY and near-superhydrophobic behaviour for 80Ni20Cr.
