Deep Learning-Based Welding Process Optimization and Adaptive Control Method for Welding Robots基于深度学习的焊接机器人焊接工艺优化与自适应控制方法

Hai-Tao Li, Tian Xiao-long, Peng Zhang, Feng Yang, Tao Wang, Ci-En Li

GMAW (gas metal arc welding) robots are frequently used in contemporary manufacturing as they offer high levels of productivity, repeatable process stability and less need for manual labour. However, even today practical robotic welding is often based upon manual design, teaching and offline programming. Welding parameters and trajectories must be redesigned whenever the workpiece, joint geometries or assembly conditions change. Furthermore, during welding, changes to the weld-seam state caused by machining error, assembly deviation and cyclic heat input result in weld parameters planned for ideal conditions becoming inadequate and the risk of incomplete fusion, porosity, cracking, slag inclusions and unstable backside formations greatly increased. In this paper a method based on deep learning is presented to provide optimization of the welding process and adaptive control for GMAW robots. This method consists of integrated quality grade oriented process planning; site-specifically improved multi-objective parameter optimization; semi-supervised semantic segmentation for the perception of weld seams and molten pools and their associated spatter suppression through neural networks predicting weld formation; and fuzzy adaptive control logic. Butt joints of Q235 carbon structural steel were tested experimentally and a response surface model was developed linking welding current, welding speed, torch angle and nozzle height to geometric properties of the welds.GMAW（熔化极气体保护电弧焊）机器人因生产效率高、工艺稳定性可重复且对人工的需求较少，被广泛应用于现代制造业。然而，即使在今天，实际的机器人焊接仍往往依赖人工设计、示教和离线编程。每当工件、接头几何形状或装配条件发生变化时，都必须重新设计焊接参数和轨迹。此外，在焊接过程中，加工误差、装配偏差和周期性热输入引起的焊缝状态变化，会使针对理想条件规划的焊接参数不再适用，并大幅增加未熔合、气孔、裂纹、夹渣和背面成形不稳定的风险。本文提出了一种基于深度学习的方法，用于GMAW机器人的焊接工艺优化与自适应控制。该方法集成了面向质量等级的工艺规划、针对现场条件改进的多目标参数优化、用于焊缝和熔池感知的半监督语义分割及通过预测焊缝成形的神经网络进行的相关飞溅抑制，以及模糊自适应控制逻辑。研究对Q235碳素结构钢对接接头进行了实验测试，并建立了将焊接电流、焊接速度、焊枪角度和喷嘴高度与焊缝几何特征相关联的响应面模型。

Xiaocui Wu, Yingjie Zhao, Huijie Zhang, Qi Cheng, Limao Wei, Yuwang Liu

This study aims to achieve high-accuracy prediction of the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of friction stir-welded A7N01P-T4 aluminum alloy joints, elucidate the effects of welding parameters on UTS, and optimize the welding process window. Rotational speed, welding speed, and shoulder plunge depth were used as input variables, with UTS as the output. Machine learning models were developed using 27 experimental samples and evaluated by leave-one-out cross-validation. Comparison of decision tree, K-nearest neighbors, and Gaussian process regression identifies decision tree as the best baseline model, with an R 2 of 0.905. An ensemble learning strategy was subsequently introduced to construct a gradient boosting decision tree, increasing R 2 to 0.966, and grid search optimization of its key hyperparameters further improves R 2 to 0.978. SHapley Additive exPlanations analysis shows that the importance of the welding parameters follows the order of welding speed, rotational speed, and shoulder plunge depth, and the identified parameter effects agree well with the experimental results and observed microstructural evolution. The optimized model predicts fully feasible ranges of all the welding parameters for a joint efficiency of at least 90%, and the shoulder plunge depth acts as a key parameter for further refining the high-strength welding process window. T. Sampath kumar, Rajaram Ganesan, M.M. Reddy

Two protective coatings, 80Ni20Cr and NiCrAlY, were deposited on Al 6061-T6 alloy substrates using the Detonation Gun (D-Gun) thermal spray process. The deposited coatings were studied for their microstructure, phase composition, surface roughness, hardness, corrosion behaviour, wettability, and solid particle erosion resistance. Characterisation was done using SEM-EDS, XRD, AFM, nanoindentation, potentiodynamic polarisation, contact angle measurement, and erosion testing. SEM-EDS results showed that both coatings bonded well to the substrate with uniform thickness. The NiCrAlY coating had a denser microstructure with lower porosity compared to 80Ni20Cr. XRD identified CrNi phases with FCC crystal structure in 80Ni20Cr and Al₂NiY phases with orthorhombic structure in NiCrAlY. Nanocrystalline grain structure was observed in both coatings. AFM measurements showed that NiCrAlY had the lowest surface roughness among all three samples. Nanoindentation results showed that NiCrAlY hardness is approximately 10 times higher than 80Ni20Cr. Specifically, NiCrAlY showed a hardness of 7895.36 MPa (731.2 HV), compared with 780.11 MPa (72.25 HV) for 80Ni20Cr and 708.68 MPa for the uncoated Al 6061 substrate. Potentiodynamic polarisation tests in 3.5 wt% NaCl solution confirmed that NiCrAlY recorded the lowest corrosion current density of 6.625 × 10 -7 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.00567 mmpy. The 80Ni20Cr coating gave a corrosion current density of 1.585 × 10 - 6 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.01427 mmpy. Both figures are considerably lower than the bare substrate at 0.5185 mmpy. Contact angle measurements confirmed superhydrophobic behaviour for NiCrAlY and near-superhydrophobic behaviour for 80Ni20Cr. Solid particle erosion test results showed that 80Ni20Cr recorded the lowest mass loss. Under the laboratory conditions used (3.5 wt% NaCl at room temperature; a single erosion condition of 90° impact angle, 40 m/s and 5 min), NiCrAlY showed the better hardness and corrosion resistance, whereas 80Ni20Cr showed the better erosion resistance. Welding quality is a critical factor in industrial equipment manufacturing, directly influencing structural integrity, operational reliability, and component safety. However, welding discontinuities may result from the interaction of technical, operational, organizational, and environmental factors, requiring integrated preventive quality management approaches. This study aims to analyze the main factors influencing weld quality in industrial equipment manufacturing, systematically classifying potential causes of discontinuities and identifying preventive measures based on technical standards. The research adopts a qualitative, exploratory, and descriptive approach, supported by a literature review and documentary analysis of welding quality standards, particularly the ABNT NBR ISO 3834 series. The Ishikawa Diagram, structured according to the 6M framework, was employed to classify potential causes into Method, Machine, Material, Manpower, Measurement, and Environment. The findings identified five major welding discontinuities: porosity, cracks, lack of fusion, incomplete penetration, and undercut. The analysis indicates that these discontinuities may arise from interconnected process conditions, emphasizing the importance of standardized procedures, equipment monitoring, appropriate material handling, personnel qualification, and systematic inspection. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. The hardness and post-scratch surface morphology of Cr₃C₂–NiCr coatings subjected to ultrasonic nanocrystal surface modification (UNSM) were examined for detonation-sprayed (DS) and high-velocity oxy-fuel (HVOF) deposits. Four treated samples per deposition route were evaluated at static loads of 20 and 30 N and vibration amplitudes of 20 and 30 µm. Instrumental hardness was measured at a load of 300 mN with a dwell time of 10 s. The DS samples exhibited hardness values of 820–970 HV, whereas the HVOF samples covered the wider range of 546–1165 HV. The highest values were recorded for DS-T1 at 20 N/20 µm and HVOF-T4 at 30 N/30 µm. SEM fields acquired after scratch testing showed heterogeneous surface contrast and crack-like discontinuities in both coating series. These observations provide qualitative evidence of the post-test surface condition but do not isolate damage caused by UNSM or establish a ranking of scratch resistance. Available mean friction coefficients are reported as supplementary observations for six treated samples.
