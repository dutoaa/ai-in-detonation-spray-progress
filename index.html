Marko Ristić, Ivana Vasovic Maksimović, Aleksandar Stepanovic, Ljubomir Nešovanović, Mirko Maksimović

Within Thermal Power Plants, the components of the ventilation mill operate under complex conditions. Despite the operation conditions of high temperatures, these components are exposed to a high level of wear due to dust and sand particles flowing through the system. This paper aims to analyse the enhancement of the wear resistance of the ventilation mill. Within this research, a CFD analysis was performed as a comprehensive tool to determine the critical zones in the system. Considering the working conditions, it is emphasized that these components are characterized by high velocity particle flows, sand concentrations reaching up to 40%, and the presence of other abrasive mineral particles. Within this research, the application of various wear-resistant coatings is analyzed through detailed experimental testing to confirm the validity of each solution and establish an optimal proposition. Applying the various deposition techniques was considered, including manual metal arc welding, high-velocity oxygen fuel (HVOF) spraying, and plasma welding, utilizing filler materials with diverse chemical compositions. Using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and Energy- Dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), the structure and distribution of chemical elements of the coatings were determined. Determination of hardness distribution across a cross-section of samples is important in selecting the coating, and in this paper, it is analyzed, and the results are presented. For the experimental testing, a special controlled sandblasting chamber was used in this research, where the working conditions are simulated with an abrasive environment, as is in ventilation mills. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量预测的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. These findings provide process-driven design guidelines for developing robust HDPE joints and demonstrate how integrating computational modeling with machine learning enables efficient optimization, offering valuable insights into the key characteristics of the FSW process in thermoplastics and supporting its broader adoption in industrial applications.搅拌摩擦焊（FSW）已被认可为连接热塑性材料的有效技术。本研究结合计算建模与机器学习方法，对高密度聚乙烯（HDPE）搅拌摩擦焊中的焊接温度、竖直轴向力、材料流动和缺陷形成等特征进行综合预测研究，并通过实验结果加以验证。研究建立了三维耦合欧拉-拉格朗日（CEL）数值模型，模拟不同转速、焊接速度和底部预热条件下的HDPE焊接过程。CEL数值模型经实验数据验证，在预测孔洞形成以及热响应和力学响应方面表现出很高的一致性。结果表明，底表面预热能够有效减小温度梯度，并稳定竖直方向的力波动。定量而言，60 °C底表面预热使峰值下压力从1600 N降至接近1100 N，并使峰值温度从未预热时的70 °C升至约100 °C，从而减缓厚度方向的温度梯度，并使轴向力分布趋于稳定。同时，利用实验数据训练了两个多层感知机人工神经网络（ANN），用于预测温度和轴向力随时间的响应，R2介于0.91至0.99之间，表明其在所测试的FSW参数空间内具有较高的准确性和较强的泛化能力。CEL计算模拟准确预测了焊接区域的形貌，包括搅拌区、热机械影响区和热影响区；而CEL–ANN联合框架能够快速生成工艺图谱，标示有缺陷和无缺陷的焊接工艺窗口。在1500 rpm、30 mm/min及60 °C预热条件下获得了最优条件，而在1000 rpm且不预热时，由于形成虫洞缺陷，观察到较差的可焊性。这些发现为制备可靠的HDPE接头提供了以工艺为导向的设计指南，并展示了计算建模与机器学习的融合如何实现高效优化，为理解热塑性材料FSW过程的关键特征提供了有价值的认识，同时支持其在工业应用中的更广泛采用。

Microstructure and surface performance of detonation gun sprayed 80Ni20Cr and NiCrAlY coatings on Al 6061-T6 substrate for structural applications面向结构应用的 Al 6061-T6 基体上爆炸喷涂 80Ni20Cr 和 NiCrAlY 涂层的微观组织与表面性能

T. Sampath kumar, Rajaram Ganesan, M.M. Reddy

Two protective coatings, 80Ni20Cr and NiCrAlY, were deposited on Al 6061-T6 alloy substrates using the Detonation Gun (D-Gun) thermal spray process. The deposited coatings were studied for their microstructure, phase composition, surface roughness, hardness, corrosion behaviour, wettability, and solid particle erosion resistance. Characterisation was done using SEM-EDS, XRD, AFM, nanoindentation, potentiodynamic polarisation, contact angle measurement, and erosion testing. SEM-EDS results showed that both coatings bonded well to the substrate with uniform thickness. The NiCrAlY coating had a denser microstructure with lower porosity compared to 80Ni20Cr. XRD identified CrNi phases with FCC crystal structure in 80Ni20Cr and Al₂NiY phases with orthorhombic structure in NiCrAlY. Nanocrystalline grain structure was observed in both coatings. AFM measurements showed that NiCrAlY had the lowest surface roughness among all three samples. Nanoindentation results showed that NiCrAlY hardness is approximately 10 times higher than 80Ni20Cr. Specifically, NiCrAlY showed a hardness of 7895.36 MPa (731.2 HV), compared with 780.11 MPa (72.25 HV) for 80Ni20Cr and 708.68 MPa for the uncoated Al 6061 substrate. Potentiodynamic polarisation tests in 3.5 wt% NaCl solution confirmed that NiCrAlY recorded the lowest corrosion current density of 6.625 × 10 -7 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.00567 mmpy. The 80Ni20Cr coating gave a corrosion current density of 1.585 × 10 - 6 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.01427 mmpy. Both figures are considerably lower than the bare substrate at 0.5185 mmpy. Contact angle measurements confirmed superhydrophobic behaviour for NiCrAlY and near-superhydrophobic behaviour for 80Ni20Cr. Solid particle erosion test results showed that 80Ni20Cr recorded the lowest mass loss. Under the laboratory conditions used (3.5 wt% NaCl at room temperature; a single erosion condition of 90° impact angle, 40 m/s and 5 min), NiCrAlY showed the better hardness and corrosion resistance, whereas 80Ni20Cr showed the better erosion resistance.
