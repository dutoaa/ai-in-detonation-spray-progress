Effect of welding parameters on tensile and impact strength of dissimilar Hardox 450–St 52 steel jointsEffect of welding parameters on tensile and impact strength of dissimilar Hardox 450–St 52 steel joints

İbrahim Biliz, Serkan Bati, Oktay Adiyaman, Musa Kilic

Abstract This study investigates the influence of welding parameters and joint configuration on the mechanical efficiency and microstructural evolution of dissimilar Hardox 450–St 52 steel joints. These materials are commonly used together in excavator and backhoe loader buckets to optimize wear performance and cost-effectiveness. The joint integrity was evaluated through optical microscopy, SEM analysis, tensile tests, and Charpy U-notch impact tests. Results indicated that the welding thermal cycle induced a distinct heat-affected zone (HAZ) and significant grain growth, leading to localized softening, particularly on the Hardox 450 side. In single-sided welds, mechanical performance deteriorated with increasing welding current. However, double-sided welding configurations partially mitigated this degradation by providing a more balanced thermal distribution and improved structural homogeneity. Notably, the double-sided specimen welded at 190 A achieved a tensile strength of 485.30 ± 1.92 MPa, nearly matching the St 52 base metal. Furthermore, the welded joints exhibited favorable energy-absorption behavior under impact loading, particularly in the double-sided configuration. Specifically, the 190 A double-sided joint reached an absorbed energy of 62 ± 0.71 J, surpassing the impact toughness of the Hardox 450 base metal (60 J). Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethyleneComputational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Dynamic Modeling of a Welding Torch Umbilical and Its Impact on Robot DynamicsDynamic Modeling of a Welding Torch Umbilical and Its Impact on Robot Dynamics

Nicolas Gautier, Yves Guillermit, Mathieu Porez, Fabien Rousset, Damien Chablat

Robotic welding is widely used in industrial manufacturing, where the welding torch is often connected to the generator through an external umbilical. With the increasing deployment of lightweight and collaborative robots, the dynamic influence of this umbilical can significantly affect the robot motion and the actuation forces. This paper proposes a constrained multibody dynamic model of a welding umbilical, represented as a serial chain of rigid bodies interconnected by passive joints with elastic and dissipative effects. Prescribed motions at the distal anchor point are introduced through holonomic kinematic constraints. The equations of motion are reduced by projecting the dynamics onto the subspace of admissible velocities, yielding an efficient formulation free of Lagrange multipliers. The reaction wrench exerted by the umbilical on the robot is explicitly recovered. Mass-balance interpretation of erosion–oxidation degradation in NiCr-based thermal spray coatings under fly ash impingement at 600 °CMass-balance interpretation of erosion–oxidation degradation in NiCr-based thermal spray coatings under fly ash impingement at 600 °C

Slamet Prasetyo Utomo, Vivi Aisah Fardilah, Hanna Zakiyya, Mochamad Arif Irfa’i

High-temperature degradation of protective thermal-spray coatings under fly ash impingement is governed by coupled oxidation, deposition, and erosion-assisted material removal. Consequently, transient mass change does not necessarily represent post-cleaning residual degradation because oxide growth, fly ash deposition, and particle embedment can partially compensate for material removal. This study introduces a paired-specimen mass-balance methodology to distinguish oxidation-related mass gain from erosion-assisted residual loss in Co-, WC-, and Mo-modified NiCr-based coatings exposed to fly ash at 600 °C. For each coating, one specimen was exposed to hot air and the paired specimen to hot air with fly ash under matched thermal histories. Time-resolved and post-cleaning mass measurements were combined with scanning electron microscopy, X-ray diffraction, and Vickers hardness to relate degradation behaviour to coating structure and properties. All coatings showed a positive pre-cleaning mass change after 600 s, while interval-resolved responses alternated between mass gain and mass loss. However, post-cleaning paired mass-balance analysis revealed substantial differences in residual response. NiCr and NiCrCo exhibited substantial apparent erosion-assisted residual loss, whereas WC-containing coatings exhibited markedly lower residual loss. NiCrWCCo showed the lowest apparent paired residual-loss response, with no additional erosion-assisted residual loss resolved relative to the oxidation reference under the present conditions; this response was consistent with its compact lamellar structure, improved inter-splat cohesion, and carbide reinforcement. The results support three mass-balance regimes: oxidation/deposition-dominant, balanced (quasi-stable), and erosion-assisted residual-loss.
