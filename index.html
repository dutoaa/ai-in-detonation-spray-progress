Mitigating Weld Defects in AISI 4145 Medium-Carbon Steel During Shielded Metal Arc Welding减少 AISI 4145 中碳钢焊条电弧焊过程中的焊接缺陷

Delelegn Toga, Seyidu Wohabrebi Abrah, Misganu Kabeta, Samuel Melese

Welding defects pose significant challenges to product quality and productivity in manufacturing industries. Shielded metal arc welding (SMAW) is the most widely used welding method in developing countries such as Ethiopia. This thesis identifies and addresses the root causes of welding defects in SMAW of medium carbon steel (AISI 4145) at Akaki Basic Metal Industry (ABMI), and optimizes welding parameters to enhance weld quality and reduce defect rates. Pareto analysis identified porosity and undercut as the most critical defects, together accounting for a 6% rejection rate in production over two years. Ishikawa (cause-and-effect) diagrams were used to analyze the root causes of these defects in detail, including incorrect welding parameters, inadequate preheat/post-heat treatment, improper technique, and inconsistent quality checks. A Taguchi L9 orthogonal array was then applied to optimize welding current, voltage, and speed. Welding current had the greatest effect on tensile strength (72.18% contribution), followed by voltage (19.73%) and speed (3.88%). Applying the optimized parameters (140 A, 40 V, 80 mm/s) reduced the defect rate from 6% to 2.7%. The study concludes that systematic optimization of welding parameters can effectively mitigate defects, improve weld quality, and enhance productivity in SMAW processes.焊接缺陷给制造业的产品质量和生产率带来了重大挑战。焊条电弧焊（SMAW）是埃塞俄比亚等发展中国家使用最广泛的焊接方法。本学位论文识别并针对 Akaki Basic Metal Industry（ABMI）中碳钢（AISI 4145）焊条电弧焊中的焊接缺陷根本原因提出解决措施，并优化焊接参数，以提高焊缝质量、降低缺陷率。帕累托分析确定，气孔和咬边是最关键的缺陷，两者合计导致了两年生产中 6% 的拒收率。研究采用石川图（因果图）详细分析了这些缺陷的根本原因，包括焊接参数不当、预热／后热处理不足、操作技术不当以及质量检查不一致。随后采用田口 L9 正交表优化焊接电流、电压和焊接速度。焊接电流对抗拉强度的影响最大（贡献率为 72.18%），其次是电压（19.73%）和焊接速度（3.88%）。采用优化参数（140 A、40 V、80 mm/s）后，缺陷率从 6% 降至 2.7%。研究得出结论：系统优化焊接参数能够有效减少缺陷、提高焊缝质量，并提升焊条电弧焊工艺的生产率。

Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Controlling molten droplet deposition and coating densification is crucial for improving the surface properties of lightweight aluminum alloys; however, the relationship between substrate temperature, splat spreading–solidification behavior, and the resulting microstructure of atmospheric plasma-sprayed (APS) composite coatings remains insufficiently understood. The key novelty of this work is to establish a multiscale relationship between the temperature-dependent behavior of individual splats and the subsequent densification and mechanical properties of the bulk coating. Establishing how individual deposition units evolve into a dense coating structure is essential for the rational control of coating microstructural and mechanical integrity. This study investigated the droplet deposition behavior, microstructural evolution, and hardness of NiCrAl-10 wt.% TiB2 coatings fabricated on 6061-T6 aluminum alloy substrates at substrate temperatures of 230, 330, and 430 °C. Increasing substrate temperature promoted splat spreading and morphological continuity by delaying solidification and extending the liquid-state lifetime. On polished substrates, the average splat diameter increased from 47.82 ± 5.4 to 57.54 ± 3.1 μm, while coating porosity decreased from 1.48 ± 0.42% to 0.55 ± 0.26%. Meanwhile, microhardness increased from 317.7 ± 3.9 to 336.3 ± 3.2 HV0.1. The dominant γ-Ni(Cr,Al) phase remained unchanged.
