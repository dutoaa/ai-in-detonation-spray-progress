Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量预测的计算与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. These findings provide process-driven design guidelines for developing robust HDPE joints and demonstrate how integrating computational modeling with machine learning enables efficient optimization, offering valuable insights into the key characteristics of the FSW process in thermoplastics and supporting its broader adoption in industrial applications.搅拌摩擦焊（FSW）已被认为是一种连接热塑性材料的有效技术。本研究综合采用计算与机器学习方法，对高密度聚乙烯（HDPE）搅拌摩擦焊中的焊接温度、垂直轴向力、材料流动和缺陷形成等特征进行预测，并通过实验结果加以验证。研究建立了三维耦合欧拉–拉格朗日（CEL）数值模型，以模拟不同转速、焊接速度及底部预热条件下的HDPE焊接过程。通过实验数据验证了CEL数值模型，其在孔洞形成以及热响应和力学响应的预测方面表现出良好的一致性。结果表明，底面预热能够有效降低温度梯度并稳定垂直力波动。定量而言，60 °C的底面预热使峰值下压力从1600 N降至接近1100 N，并使峰值温度从未预热时的70 °C升至约100 °C，从而减缓厚度方向的温度梯度并稳定轴向力分布。同时，利用实验数据训练了两个多层感知机人工神经网络（ANN），以预测温度和轴向力随时间的响应，R2介于0.91和0.99之间，表明其在所测试的FSW参数空间内具有较高的精度和较强的泛化能力。CEL计算模拟准确预测了焊接区域的形貌，包括搅拌区、热机械影响区和热影响区；而CEL–ANN联合框架能够快速生成工艺图，标示出存在缺陷和无缺陷的焊接工艺窗口。其中，在1500 rpm、30 mm/min和60 °C预热条件下获得了最佳工艺条件，而在1000 rpm且不预热时，由于形成隧道型缺陷，焊接性较差。这些发现为制备可靠的HDPE接头提供了基于工艺的设计指导，并展示了计算建模与机器学习的融合如何实现高效优化，为理解热塑性材料FSW过程的关键特征提供了有价值的见解，同时支持该技术在工业应用中的更广泛采用。

Invar steel has an extremely low coefficient of thermal expansion, making it widely used in the manufacturing of molds for aerospace composite materials. Most existing studies focus on process aspects, lacking in-depth analysis of the fluid dynamics behavior of the molten pool. Therefore, numerical simulation and high-speed photography were employed to analyze the effects of different process parameters on the molten pool morphology evolution and arc characteristics during laser-TIG hybrid welding of Invar steel, which provides crucial theoretical support for the optimization of the Invar steel welding process. The results indicate that within the laser power range of 5 ~ 6 kW, the increased power leads to greater recoil pressure, which promotes a larger penetration depth and keyhole dimension, intensifies the overall molten pool flow, and makes the arc prone to being attracted by the laser and deflected at a high power. Within the welding speed range of 0.5 ~ 0.8 m/min, a higher speed reduces the heat input, leading to a decreased penetration depth and weld width, and an increased flow velocity and cooling rate of the molten pool; additionally, the arc is prone to deformation at a high speed.
