Microstructural Evolution and Fatigue Performance of Welded Joints in Thick-Wall Pressure Vessels厚壁压力容器焊接接头的微观组织演变与疲劳性能

Yan Long Ding, Jin Qiang Zhao, Xin Hui Wang, Heng Li, Ming Ming Zhang, Xin Guang Du

Thick-wall welded pressure vessels operate under combinations of internal pressure, thermal cycling, structural restraint, corrosive or hydrogen-containing environments, and long service durations. In such components, fatigue performance is not governed by a single nominal material property but by a manufacturing-dependent internal state comprising spatially variable microstructure, hardness, local constitutive response, and residual stress. This review critically synthesizes the mechanisms by which welding processes and post-weld heat treatment determine fatigue performance in thick-wall pressure-vessel joints, with emphasis on SA508-type reactor pressure-vessel steels and 2.25Cr-1Mo- 0.25V/Cr-Mo-V steels used in petrochemical and hydrogenation reactors. Conventional multi-pass arc welding, narrow-gap welding, electron-beam welding, and laser-based high-energy-density processes are interpreted as distinct thermal-history generators. Their associated peak temperatures, cooling rates, reheating cycles, and through-thickness thermal gradients control fusion-zone solidification, HAZ subdivision, prior-austenite grain evolution, martensite-austenite constituent formation, carbide/carbonitride precipitation, segregation, and local mechanical mismatch. The review further evaluates how welding residual stresses originate from thermal contraction, plasticity, phase-transformation strain, restraint, and wall-thickness effects, and how post-weld heat treatment redistributes stress while simultaneously modifying phase stability and precipitate morphology. Fatigue crack initiation and propagation are discussed as coupled microstructure-residual-stress phenomena involving cyclic strain localization, small-crack interaction with microstructural barriers, crack-tip constraint, and environmental acceleration. Advanced characterization methods, including EBSD, SEM/TEM/APT, neutron diffraction, nanoindentation, and small punch testing, are assessed as enabling tools for spatially resolved microstructure-property mapping. Finally, thermometallurgical- mechanical simulation, crystal plasticity, representative-volume-element modelling, fracture mechanics, physics-informed machine learning, and digital-twin frameworks are synthesized into a process-microstructure-stress-fatigue roadmap for predictive integrity management. Shrikant Vishwanath Joshi

Abstract Solution Precursor Plasma Spraying (SPPS), wherein dissolved metal salt precursors are injected into a plasma jet to synthesize diverse functional coatings, offers compelling advantages over conventional thermal spray techniques. These include elimination of powder synthesis, use of aqueous precursor feedstock capable of achieving molecular-level compositional control, and rapid one-step synthesis of coatings with chemistries for which spray-grade powders are readily unavailable, such as high-entropy oxides, perovskites, and related complex oxides. Despite these strengths and an encouraging body of prior research, SPPS research has declined from its modest peak in the mid-2010s. This is attributable primarily to the prohibitively narrow processing windows and low deposition rates imposed by legacy thermal spray hardware. This perspective argues that the advent of high-power axially-fed plasma spray systems fundamentally changes the technological landscape for SPPS. Axial injection delivers symmetric, near-complete thermal energy utilization, broader process windows, and deposition rates of practical interest in early demonstrations, thereby addressing the above limitations at laboratory scale. Emerging axial SPPS results across diverse material systems already demonstrate coating microstructures and performance metrics that rival suspension plasma spray, validating the broader promise of this approach. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation.
