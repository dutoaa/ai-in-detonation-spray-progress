Rank reduction autoencoders for mechanical design: Learning structured latent manifolds for topology optimization面向机械设计的降秩自编码器：学习用于拓扑优化的结构化潜在流形

Ismael Ben-Yelun, Mohammed El Fallaki Idrissi, Jad Mounayer, Sebastian Rodriguez, Francisco Chinesta

This work presents a data-driven framework for fast forward and inverse analyses in topology optimization by combining Rank Reduction Autoencoders with neural latent-space mappings. The methodology targets the efficient approximation of the relationship between optimized geometries and their corresponding mechanical responses or quantities of interest with a particular focus on compliance-minimized linear elastic structures. High-dimensional topology optimization results are first compressed using Rank Reduction Autoencoders, which encode the data into low-dimensional manifolds via Singular Value Decomposition, thus extracting the most important (latent) features. Separate Rank Reduction Autoencoder models are trained for geometry and for different types of quantities of interest, including scalar metrics, one-dimensional stress fields, and full two-dimensional von Mises stress distributions. The resulting low-dimensional latent manifolds are then related through multilayer perceptrons to address both direct problems – predicting structural responses from geometry – and inverse problems—recovering geometries from prescribed performance targets. The proposed approach is demonstrated on a half MBB beam and a cantilever beam, with the latter studied under separate and simultaneous variations in volume fraction and load position, using datasets generated via density-based Solid Isotropic Material with Penalization optimization. Numerical results show that the framework enables accurate and computationally efficient surrogate models, with increasing robustness and fidelity as richer quantities of interest are considered. The methodology also shows potential for generative mechanical design through preliminary examples of new geometries and response synthesis via latent-space exploration.本研究将降秩自编码器与神经网络潜在空间映射相结合，提出了一种用于拓扑优化中快速正向与逆向分析的数据驱动框架。该方法旨在高效近似优化后的几何形状与其相应力学响应或关注量之间的关系，重点关注柔度最小化的线弹性结构。首先，使用降秩自编码器压缩高维拓扑优化结果，通过奇异值分解将数据编码到低维流形中，从而提取最重要的（潜在）特征。针对几何形状和不同类型的关注量，分别训练独立的降秩自编码器模型；这些关注量包括标量指标、一维应力场以及完整的二维 von Mises 应力分布。随后，通过多层感知机建立所得低维潜在流形之间的联系，以同时解决正向问题——根据几何形状预测结构响应，以及逆向问题——根据给定的性能目标反求几何形状。所提出的方法在半 MBB 梁和悬臂梁上进行了演示，其中针对悬臂梁研究了体积分数与载荷位置分别变化和同时变化的情况，所用数据集通过基于密度的固体各向同性材料惩罚（Solid Isotropic Material with Penalization）优化生成。数值结果表明，该框架能够构建准确且计算高效的代理模型，并且随着所考虑的关注量信息更加丰富，其鲁棒性与保真度也随之提高。通过潜在空间探索生成新几何形状和合成响应的初步示例，该方法还展示了其在生成式机械设计中的潜力。

Dastan Buitkenov, Nurmakhanbet Raisov, Laila Sulyubayeva, A.Y. Zhakupova, Daryn Baizhan, Nurbol Berdimuratov

This study investigates the effect of laser remelting on the microstructure, phase composition, mechanical properties, tribological behavior, and corrosion resistance of self-fluxing NiCrFeBSiC coatings deposited onto AISI 321 stainless steel by detonation spraying. The coatings were characterized before and after laser treatment using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), X-ray diffraction (XRD), instrumented indentation, adhesion testing, ball-on-disk tribometry, and potentiodynamic polarization measurements. SEM analysis revealed that laser remelting reduced coating porosity from 0.306 ± 0.014% to 0.15 ± 0.09%, accompanied by microstructural densification and a reduction in coating thickness. XRD analysis confirmed the preservation of the principal crystalline phases, including Ni3Fe, Cr7C3, CrB, and Ni3B, following laser treatment. The Vickers hardness increased from 1049.1 ± 43.4 to 1206.1 ± 63.1 HV, while the elastic modulus increased from 233.6 ± 6.2 to 258.4 ± 5.3 GPa. Furthermore, adhesion strength increased by 19.4%, whereas surface roughness decreased by 19.2%. Laser remelting significantly improved tribological performance, reducing the average coefficient of friction from 0.535 ± 0.048 to 0.409 ± 0.086 and the specific wear rate from 2.864 × 10−4 to 1.447 × 10−4 mm3/(N·m), corresponding to a 49.5% reduction in wear. Electrochemical measurements indicated a modest decrease in corrosion current density of approximately 3.0%, accompanied by changes in the morphology of corrosion-affected regions. The improvements are associated with laser-induced microstructural densification, redistribution of alloying elements, and preservation of hard reinforcing phases. Abstract Friction stir welding is an advanced joining process widely used for its superior weld quality, high seam strength, and minimal distortion. Therefore, it is widely applied in industries that require these characteristics, such as the aerospace and the automotive sector (battery trays for electric vehicles). Nevertheless, process irregularities may occur, leading to defects that are often visible on the weld seam surface. For this reason, the weld seam surface is typically inspected after welding by means of human visual inspection. This requires well-trained personnel and involves challenges such as detailed documentation and time-consuming evaluation, particularly when multiple or long weld seams are examined. To address the limitations of post-process inspection, inline quality monitoring systems are gaining increasing importance. Several studies have investigated surface quality monitoring approaches, often in combination with image classification models. The present work contributes to automated surface quality monitoring by investigating the use of modern convolutional neural networks in a pixel-level approach (semantic segmentation). Segmentation enables the identification, localization, and quantification of surface defects. In this study, promising results were obtained for the segmentation of flash, surface galling, and surface lack of fill, with a global accuracy of 97.69% and a mean intersection over union (mIoU) of 83.78%. An IoU of 79.18% was achieved for flash, while the IoUs for surface lack of fill and surface galling were 80.22% and 66.87%, respectively. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. The present study investigates the effect of flame-spraying process parameters on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Ni–Cr–B–Si-based self-fluxing coatings.
