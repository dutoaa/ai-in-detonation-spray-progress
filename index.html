Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethyleneComputational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Megavannan Mani

The growing adoption of lightweight, corrosion-resistant structures has increased the use of dissimilar stainless-steel joints in transportation, manufacturing, and structural applications, necessitating reliable process optimization for sustainable resistance spot welding (RSW). This study investigates the welding behaviour of 1.2 mm thick SS304 and SS420 stainless-steel sheets using a mixed-level full-factorial design to evaluate the influence of welding current (7–10 kA), welding time (6–8 cycles), and electrode force (3–6 kN) on joint performance. Apparent lap-shear stress was determined from the measured bonded nugget area to provide a representative assessment of joint strength. Analysis of variance indicated that welding current was the most influential parameter, contributing 87.44% of the total variation, followed by welding time (8.84%) and electrode force (4.56%). The optimum welding condition of 10 kA, 8 cycles, and 5 kN achieved a maximum failure load of 8.58 kN with an apparent lap-shear stress of 437 MPa. Macrostructural observations revealed a transition from interfacial fracture to pull-out failure with indicating improved nugget development and joint integrity. Robust Monitoring of Arc Welding Processes: A Generalizable Framework with DVAE and Particle FilterRobust Monitoring of Arc Welding Processes: A Generalizable Framework with DVAE and Particle Filter

Yue Cao, Hai Lin, YuMing Zhang

Arc welding processes are essential for continuous fabrication but prone to disturbances that impair weld quality, making real-time monitoring critical yet difficult due to complex visual patterns and nonlinear, time-varying dynamics. Deep learning shows promise but faces scalability limits because of its dependence on large labeled datasets and application-specific tuning. We explore whether a unified approach can characterize major arc welding processes across applications and improve scalability through consistent state monitoring. This paper introduces a robust and generalizable monitoring framework for arc welding. It combines unsupervised deep latent representation learning, which extracts compact features from weld pool images, with Bayesian filtering to handle persistent and fluctuating disturbances such as arc radiation and specular reflections. Specifically, a Dynamic Variational Autoencoder (DVAE), consisting of a CNN-based encoder-decoder and an LSTM-based transition model, jointly learns latent representations and their evolution under control inputs. For robust real-time inference, a specialized Particle Filter (PF) propagates the latent and LSTM hidden states, preserving process history while suppressing sensor noise. This design is well suited to welding's slow and inertial dynamics. Georg Hauptmann, Gean Henrique Marcatto de Oliveira, Himal Ammay, Sebastian Fritsche, Athanasios Toumpis, Alexander Galloway

As a solid-state joining process, refill friction stir spot welding (RFSSW or refill FSSW) has gained increasing importance for welding dissimilar materials due to its notable advantages over conventional fusion-based welding methods. The welding process reduces common issues such as hot (solidification) cracking as well as molten-state-related porosities while producing high-strength joints. The process is also more environmentally friendly as it does not require filler materials and produces no fumes. Despite these benefits, challenges such as increased tool wear and longer processing times still exist for dissimilar and similar welds. This review provides a comprehensive insight into RFSSW of dissimilar metals, starting with an introduction to the process and its key parameters. Following this review, an examination of the available literature highlights the influence of process parameters on weld quality, focusing on mechanical properties and corrosion behavior. This is particularly important because dissimilar material combinations exhibit differences in electrochemical potential. Additionally, recent developments in process modeling are summarized. The work concludes by addressing the potential applications of RFSSW for dissimilar structures.
