The framework represents a student-built circuit as a series safety function whose integrity is degraded by contact welding, and derives the conditions under which a welded contact converts a demanded stop into an undetected loss of safety function. A replication-based evaluation of the before-after-control-impact estimator was conducted under a declared variance structure with a between-cluster standard deviation of 6.0, a residual standard deviation of 4.5 and an intraclass correlation of 0.64, across 2000 replications per design cell for cluster counts of 8, 16, 24 and 32 and effect sizes of 3, 5 and 8 percentage points. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Cermet coating, which is a blend of ceramic hardness and metallic toughness, is mostly applied to enhance the wear and corrosion resistance of metals. Detonation gun ( D -gun) spraying is one of the most promising thermal spray technologies that would produce a high-quality ceramic coating. Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr coating is acceptable because of its superior mechanical characteristics and corrosion characteristic. The effectiveness of D -gun sprayed Cr₃C₂–NiCr coatings was evaluated under aggressive acidic conditions using 1 M H₂SO₄, which serves as a standardized laboratory electrolyte for assessing corrosion resistance relevant to industrial acidic environments, including the key process variables such as spraying distance, which is critically important in the determination of other coating properties such as porosity, adhesion, roughness, and hardness. Duplex stainless steel (DSS) 2205 has been sprayed with Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr at three spraying distances; 170 mm, 180 mm and 190 mm in an attempt to conduct the research on the behavior of Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr in acid conditions. Electrochemical activity of the coating was evaluated in 1 M H 2 SO 4 solutions at room temperature; by open circuit potential (OCP), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy and potentiodynamic polarisation. Investigation of surface morphology under SEM revealed dense lamellar coatings with porosity decreasing from 2.84% at 170 mm to 1.38% at 190 mm, indicating improved particle melting and coating compactness. The coating deposited at 190 mm exhibited the best electrochemical performance, with lowest corrosion rate (0.113 mmpy), compared with the uncoated substrate (2.60 mmpy). All coated specimens had a homogenous and compact coating structure. The results demonstrate that 190 mm is the optimum spray distance for producing high-integrity Cr₃C₂–NiCr coatings on DSS 2205, providing enhanced corrosion protection for components operating in aggressive acidic industrial environments. This work demonstrates that optimizing the D -gun spray distance significantly influences the microstructural integrity and electrochemical corrosion behaviour of Cr₃C₂–25NiCr coatings on DSS 2205.
