Microstructure, Sliding Wear, and Electrochemical Corrosion of a High-Entropy Alloy–Cermet Composite Thermal Spray Coating高熵合金-金属陶瓷复合热喷涂涂层的显微组织、滑动磨损与电化学腐蚀

Stavros Kiape, Anthoula Poulia, Dimitrios Nousias, Ε. Georgatis, Spyros Kamnis, Theodore E. Matikas

This study investigates the design, microstructure, and performance profile of a novel composite coating combining a high-entropy alloy (HEA) matrix with cermet reinforcement. A 50wt.%CoCrFeMnNi0.8V–50wt.% Cr3C2-Ni80Cr20 powder mixture was successfully deposited onto steel substrates via high-velocity oxy-fuel (HVOF) thermal spraying. Microstructural analysis revealed a highly dense, well-bonded coating architecture (450–500 μm thick) where partially melted, spherical HEA splats were uniformly surrounded by the Cr3C2-Ni80Cr20 phase. X-ray diffraction confirmed a complex multiphase evolution consisting of FCC, BCC, and σ-NiCr phases driven by the rapid solidification inherent to the HVOF process. Tribological evaluations via ball-on-disc testing demonstrated that incorporating the Cr3C2-Ni80Cr20 reinforcement significantly improves wear resistance compared to the monolithic HEA coating. The composite’s wear behavior is governed by a synergistic mechanism: the ductile HEA matrix accommodates plastic deformation, while the harder carbide particles enhance load-bearing capacity, transitioning from adhesive wear to mild third-body abrasion and protective tribo-oxidation. Conversely, electrochemical testing in a 3.5 wt.% NaCl solution showed that the composite coating exhibits higher corrosion current densities (10.53 × 10−6 A/cm2) and more active corrosion potentials than the pure HEA matrix. Overall, the novel composite coating offers a compelling, sustainable alternative for surface engineering applications requiring a balanced trade-off between mechanical toughness and acceptable environmental durability. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于高密度聚乙烯摩擦搅拌焊中焊缝质量预测的计算与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Arc welding processes are essential for continuous fabrication but prone to disturbances that impair weld quality, making real-time monitoring critical yet difficult due to complex visual patterns and nonlinear, time-varying dynamics. Deep learning shows promise but faces scalability limits because of its dependence on large labeled datasets and application-specific tuning. To solve the susceptibility of TC4 titanium alloys to high-temperature oxidation and subsequent performance degradation, AlCuFe quasicrystalline (QC) coatings were successfully fabricated via detonation spraying (DS) as a functional protective barrier. The oxidation kinetics, phase evolution, and mechanical stability of the coating system were systematically investigated at 450°C in an atmospheric environment for up to 250 h. The results indicate that the oxidation kinetics strictly obey the parabolic growth law, yielding a steady-state parabolic rate constant ( k p ) of 1.83 × 10 −2 mg 2 ⋅ cm −4 ⋅ h −1 . Notably, the increased density of phase boundaries and intermetallic interfaces within the multi-phase network effectively sustains the mechanical performance despite the decline in the primary QC fraction.
