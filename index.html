Friction stir welding (FSW) is an effective joining approach for dissimilar base metals, such as aluminum alloys (AA) and advanced high-strength steels. However, tool degradation during the process can compromise weld quality, shorten tool life, and increase production costs. This study evaluates the performance and durability enhancement of the tool through the FSW of AA6022-T4 and galvanized DP600 steel by applying Cr 3 C 2 -Ti-DLC thin-film coatings on H13 tool steel and WC-10% Co tools. The surface finish quality of the welds was quantitatively evaluated using a 3D white light interferometer, while real-time thermal profiles were captured using an infrared thermography camera. Tool degradation patterns and performance were assessed through optical microscopy, SEM coupled with EDS, and precise weight loss measurements. The uncoated H13 tool exhibited significant degradation, including thread flattening, micro-abrasion, and material pickup. In contrast, the uncoated WC-10% Co tool demonstrated improved wear resistance. The deposition of thin-film surface coatings significantly reduced tool degradation by 26% for H13 and 23% for WC-10% Co tools in comparison with uncoated tools. Metal matrix composite coatings with polymer fillers are a rapidly developing class of multifunctional materials that combine the mechanical strength, electrical conductivity, and corrosion resistance of metals with the low coefficient of friction, chemical inertness, and functional versatility of polymers. This review provides a systematic analysis of metal matrix polymer composite (MMPC) coatings, covering their classification according to the metallic matrix material (Ni, Cu, Co, Al, Ti, and Zn), the type of polymer filler (fluoropolymers, thermosetting resins, biopolymers, and electrically conductive polymers), and filler morphology (particles, fibers, and microcapsules). The main fabrication technologies are considered in detail, including electrodeposition, electroless deposition, thermal spraying, and cold spray, as well as hybrid and additive methods, with a comparative analysis of their advantages, limitations, and fields of application. The effects of filler composition, particle size and concentration, and the nature of the metal–polymer interface on the structure and functional properties of the coatings are discussed, including tribological characteristics, corrosion resistance, mechanical properties, and specific functionalities such as superhydrophobicity, anti-icing, biocompatibility, and self-healing. Unlike most existing reviews, which focus either on a single fabrication method, typically electrodeposition, or on a specific metal/polymer pair, this work provides a comprehensive cross-comparison of all key technologies, including hybrid approaches and additive manufacturing. The review also systematizes unresolved challenges, including weak adhesion at the metal/polymer interface, limitations in thermal resistance, difficulties in scaling up production, and the lack of standardized testing methods, while outlining promising directions such as nanostructured fillers, self-healing systems, and environmentally friendly manufacturing technologies. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Detonation gun ( D -gun) spraying is one of the most promising thermal spray technologies that would produce a high-quality ceramic coating. Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr coating is acceptable because of its superior mechanical characteristics and corrosion characteristic. The effectiveness of D -gun sprayed Cr₃C₂–NiCr coatings was evaluated under aggressive acidic conditions using 1 M H₂SO₄, which serves as a standardized laboratory electrolyte for assessing corrosion resistance relevant to industrial acidic environments, including the key process variables such as spraying distance, which is critically important in the determination of other coating properties such as porosity, adhesion, roughness, and hardness. Duplex stainless steel (DSS) 2205 has been sprayed with Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr at three spraying distances; 170 mm, 180 mm and 190 mm in an attempt to conduct the research on the behavior of Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr in acid conditions.
