Abstract Resistance spot welding (RSW) has become a widely adopted joining technique in the automotive and structural manufacturing industries due to its high productivity, potential for automation and cost-effectiveness. However, conventional joining using single-pulse RSW frequently suffers from unstable nugget formation, expulsion, excessive heat accumulation and uneven mechanical performance, particularly in dissimilar material joints and advanced high-strength steels (AHSS). The Double-Plus Resistance Spot Welding (DP-RSW) method has emerged as an improvement where advanced current modulation approach enhances the quality of the joints formed by using a pre-heating pulse, an inter-pulse cooling phase and a subsequent high-current main pulse. This review critically examines the operating principle of DP-RSW process, temperature behavior, and properties of the weldments. A comprehensive discussion is presented of the impact of DP-RSW on nugget formation, tensile shear strength, failure energy, microstructural evolution and hardness distribution. When compared to conventional single-pulse welding it was found that DP-RSW provided superior temperature control, greater nugget consistency, decreased expulsion, narrower heat-affected zones and improved mechanical performance. Further, the applicability of DP-RSW in welding AHSS, stainless steels and dissimilar steel combinations used in lightweight automotive structures have been highlighted. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于高密度聚乙烯摩擦搅拌焊焊接质量预测的计算与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr coating is acceptable because of its superior mechanical characteristics and corrosion characteristic. The effectiveness of D -gun sprayed Cr₃C₂–NiCr coatings was evaluated under aggressive acidic conditions using 1 M H₂SO₄, which serves as a standardized laboratory electrolyte for assessing corrosion resistance relevant to industrial acidic environments, including the key process variables such as spraying distance, which is critically important in the determination of other coating properties such as porosity, adhesion, roughness, and hardness.
