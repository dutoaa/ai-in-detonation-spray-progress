Abstract A FeCoCrNiAl high-entropy alloy (HEA) coating was deposited on a Ti-6Al-4V (TC4) alloy substrate by high-velocity oxygen fuel (HVOF) thermal spraying to enhance its sliding wear performance. Reciprocating sliding tests were conducted from 25 to 300 °C, and the temperature-dependent friction behavior, wear morphology, and damage evolution were examined. Meanwhile, an Archard-based finite element model incorporating the arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian adaptive mesh technique was established to reproduce the progressive wear process. The coating exhibited a compact lamellar microstructure, a BCC-dominated phase constitution, and higher hardness than the TC4 substrate. Although friction and wear increased with temperature for both materials, the coated specimens consistently showed lower coefficients of friction and less severe surface damage. Compared with the TC4 substrate, the HEA coating reduced the maximum wear depth and wear volume by 30.7% and 23.7%, respectively, at 25 °C, while the corresponding reductions reached 41.2% and 40.8% at 300 °C. Notably, the maximum reductions of 49.3% and 42.4%, respectively, were obtained at 200 °C, demonstrating that the protective effect of the coating became more pronounced at elevated temperatures. The wear of TC4 evolved from ploughing and plastic deformation to oxidation-assisted fatigue damage, whereas the coating mainly underwent mild abrasion, oxidation, and localized inter-splat delamination. The simulated wear profiles agreed well with the experimental results, with deviations below 5%, and revealed a reduced stress concentration in the coated specimens. Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. The highest tensile strength obtained for the LAFS welds was 193 MPa, which corresponds to approximately 88% of the tensile strength of the Al base metal. Two protective coatings, 80Ni20Cr and NiCrAlY, were deposited on Al 6061-T6 alloy substrates using the Detonation Gun (D-Gun) thermal spray process. The deposited coatings were studied for their microstructure, phase composition, surface roughness, hardness, corrosion behaviour, wettability, and solid particle erosion resistance. Characterisation was done using SEM-EDS, XRD, AFM, nanoindentation, potentiodynamic polarisation, contact angle measurement, and erosion testing. SEM-EDS results showed that both coatings bonded well to the substrate with uniform thickness. The NiCrAlY coating had a denser microstructure with lower porosity compared to 80Ni20Cr. XRD identified CrNi phases with FCC crystal structure in 80Ni20Cr and Al₂NiY phases with orthorhombic structure in NiCrAlY. Nanocrystalline grain structure was observed in both coatings. AFM measurements showed that NiCrAlY had the lowest surface roughness among all three samples. Nanoindentation results showed that NiCrAlY hardness is approximately 10 times higher than 80Ni20Cr. Specifically, NiCrAlY showed a hardness of 7895.36 MPa (731.2 HV), compared with 780.11 MPa (72.25 HV) for 80Ni20Cr and 708.68 MPa for the uncoated Al 6061 substrate. Potentiodynamic polarisation tests in 3.5 wt% NaCl solution confirmed that NiCrAlY recorded the lowest corrosion current density of 6.625 × 10 -7 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.00567 mmpy. The 80Ni20Cr coating gave a corrosion current density of 1.585 × 10 - 6 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.01427 mmpy. Both figures are considerably lower than the bare substrate at 0.5185 mmpy. To address this, the present study focuses on optimizing process parameters in Direct Drive Friction Welding (DDFW) for AA2024-T351. A novel drilled-to-bossed geometry, inspired by the traditional mortise-and-tenon joint, is introduced to enhance mechanical interlocking and improve weld integrity. Experimental trials were systematically designed using an L18 orthogonal array to evaluate both tensile and torsional strength. The experiments were conducted on a customized engine lathe, with friction pressure, forging pressure, spindle speed, faying surface geometry, and friction time selected as the controllable process parameters. In parallel, advanced deep learning models, including an ensemble residual network, a compact attention network, and an adaptive multiscale network, were implemented to predict ultimate tensile strength based on the welding conditions. The results revealed that the optimal parameter combination of 30 MPa friction pressure, 70 MPa forging pressure, 2200 rpm spindle speed, drilled-to-bossed geometry, and 4 min of friction time yielded exceptional mechanical performance, achieving a tensile strength of 538 MPa and torsional strength of 325 MPa. The drilled-to-bossed configuration demonstrated significantly higher joint strength compared to conventional flat-to-flat joints.
