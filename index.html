Feasibility study of using magnetic and thermoelectric methods for the assessment of storage-induced changes in flux-cored welding wires利用磁性和热电方法评估药芯焊丝储存诱导变化的可行性研究

Aleksandra Świerczyńska, Balázs Varbai, Leszek Piotrowski

The lack of knowledge regarding whether a welding consumable remains suitable for the welding process or has undergone degradation that precludes its further use constitutes a significant challenge. Appropriate control procedures and effective evaluation methods may contribute to addressing this issue. This study presents the results of investigations concerning the applicability of magnetic and thermoelectric methods for such an assessment. Rutile flux-cored welding wires were stored in four different locations and subsequently collected for testing after 3, 6, and 12 months. Their suitability for industrial application was initially assessed on the basis of visual examination. Subsequently, measurements of thermoelectric power, characterization of bulk magnetic properties, and Magnetic Barkhausen Noise (MBN) were performed for each wire. Visual inspection confirmed the good surface condition of the wires despite the exceedance of the recommended limits of temperature and relative humidity during storage. The thermoelectric measurements showed that storage environment significantly affects the Seebeck Coefficient (SC) of welding wires, with marine conditions inducing rapid changes and less aggressive environments producing a gradual time dependent increase. Magnetic property measurements indicated that two of the evaluated parameters, namely the amplitude of MBN and the area under the envelopes of MBN, exhibited changes with storage time at each exposure site. Statistical analysis demonstrated that the parameters recorded during magnetic and thermoelectric measurements were characterized by a high degree of correlation, exceeding 0.9 in some cases. A strong linear relationship between the MBN Amplitude-to-Envelope Ratio and SC was observed for wires stored in the Urban Area and Workshop environments (r = 0.99 for both), indicating a close correlation between the evolution of their thermoelectric and magnetic properties during storage. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊缝质量预测的计算与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Roshan Kuruvila, Mebin T Kuruvila, Manu Harilal, S. Thirumalai Kumaran

Cermet coating, which is a blend of ceramic hardness and metallic toughness, is mostly applied to enhance the wear and corrosion resistance of metals. Detonation gun ( D -gun) spraying is one of the most promising thermal spray technologies that would produce a high-quality ceramic coating. Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr coating is acceptable because of its superior mechanical characteristics and corrosion characteristic. The effectiveness of D -gun sprayed Cr₃C₂–NiCr coatings was evaluated under aggressive acidic conditions using 1 M H₂SO₄, which serves as a standardized laboratory electrolyte for assessing corrosion resistance relevant to industrial acidic environments, including the key process variables such as spraying distance, which is critically important in the determination of other coating properties such as porosity, adhesion, roughness, and hardness. Duplex stainless steel (DSS) 2205 has been sprayed with Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr at three spraying distances; 170 mm, 180 mm and 190 mm in an attempt to conduct the research on the behavior of Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr in acid conditions. Electrochemical activity of the coating was evaluated in 1 M H 2 SO 4 solutions at room temperature; by open circuit potential (OCP), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy and potentiodynamic polarisation. Investigation of surface morphology under SEM revealed dense lamellar coatings with porosity decreasing from 2.84% at 170 mm to 1.38% at 190 mm, indicating improved particle melting and coating compactness. The coating deposited at 190 mm exhibited the best electrochemical performance, with lowest corrosion rate (0.113 mmpy), compared with the uncoated substrate (2.60 mmpy). All coated specimens had a homogenous and compact coating structure. The results demonstrate that 190 mm is the optimum spray distance for producing high-integrity Cr₃C₂–NiCr coatings on DSS 2205, providing enhanced corrosion protection for components operating in aggressive acidic industrial environments. This work demonstrates that optimizing the D -gun spray distance significantly influences the microstructural integrity and electrochemical corrosion behaviour of Cr₃C₂–25NiCr coatings on DSS 2205. Process Parameter Optimization in Refill Friction Stir Spot Welding of 6061-T6 Aluminum Alloy and AZ31B-H24 Magnesium Alloy Dissimilar Joints6061-T6铝合金与AZ31B-H24镁合金异种接头搅拌摩擦点焊中工艺参数优化

Dan Cătălin Bîrsan, Vasile Başliu

Lightweight multi-material structures that pair aluminum with magnesium are increasingly sought in the transport and aerospace sectors, yet sound dissimilar Al–Mg joints remain difficult to obtain because the two metals readily form hard, brittle Al–Mg intermetallic compounds (IMCs) whose growth along the bond line degrades joint strength. Refill friction stir spot welding (RFSSW) is a keyhole-free, solid-state process well suited to limiting this reaction, but a validated, quantitative process window for the AA6061-T6/AZ31B-H24 couple is still lacking. This study addresses that gap by establishing quantitative correlations between the governing RFSSW parameters, namely tool rotational speed (1800–2400 rpm), sleeve penetration depth (2.2–2.6 mm) and welding time (4–6 s), and the resulting thermal field, joint integrity and mechanical performance. A two-dimensional axisymmetric, fully coupled thermomechanical finite element model was developed in Simufact Forming 2025.2 software and validated against embedded K-type thermocouple and infrared thermography measurements, with the predicted and measured peaks agreeing to within about 5% (168 °C at 4 s and 216 °C at 6 s, recorded 5 mm from the weld center). The validated model predicts stir-zone peak temperatures of 320–498 °C across the process window; these remain below the solidus of both base alloys (582 °C for AA6061-T6 and 566 °C for AZ31B-H24), confirming a bulk solid-state process, while at the hottest settings, the interfacial temperature locally exceeds the Al–Mg eutectic range (437–450 °C), allowing transient eutectic liquation confined to the faying surface.
