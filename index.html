Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethyleneComputational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. To overcome the high reflectivity and thermal conductivity of aluminum alloys, a green laser with a wavelength of 515 nm was utilized to weld 0.7 mm thick Al3003-H14 sheets. Precise joint alignment was achieved using the Auto Position Alignment (APA) function of the laser scanner. The straight pattern exhibited a 9.1% higher tensile strength compared to the wobble pattern, which is attributed to the narrower bead width, reduced heat-affected zone (HAZ), and higher joint hardness of the straight path. Comparative Study of Machine Learning Algorithms to Classify Acoustic Data of an Ultrasonic Welding ProcessComparative Study of Machine Learning Algorithms to Classify Acoustic Data of an Ultrasonic Welding Process

Timo Reindl, Peter M.-Y. Chung, Christian Bonten, Marc Kreutzbruck

Ultrasonic welding is a recognized and widely used process for joining plastic components. Particular features include localized heating of the joining partners, very short welding times, and high process efficiency. The weld seam quality in the process depends on numerous factors. The process is significantly influenced by the process parameters and the tolerances of the parts to be joined. At the same time, welding theories and material models are reaching their limits in the field of ultrasonic welding, which can lead to inconsistent process qualities. Up until now, the weld seam quality is typically assessed by subsequent destructive testing of the weld seam. We propose an acoustic monitoring of the ultrasonic welding process that allows predictions to be made about the weld seam quality during the process. In the studies presented here, the acoustic monitoring of the mechanical vibration effects occurring in the process is examined using piezo elements, a laser microphone, and a laser vibrometer. The article addresses physical mechanisms of nonlinearity in ultrasonic welding, the measurement and test setup for data acquisition as well as the evaluation procedure. In addition to the nonlinear vibration behavior occurring in the process, the weld seam quality is characterized using conventional tensile tests. Based on the obtained data, a dataset is prepared and classification according to the resulting weld seam quality is examined using machine learning algorithms. Due to the superior chemical resistance of Ethylene chlorotrifluoroethylene(ECTFE) compared to conventional thermoplastics materials, ECTFE piping has recently gained increasing attention as an alternative to polypropylene(PP) in alkaline and ozone wastewater piping systems. This piping systems relies on flange connections when installing on-site piping due to the absence of standardized butt fusion welding methods. This limitation results in increased material consumption, including stub flanges, backing rings, and gaskets, as well as additional spool fabrication and welding points, leading to higher construction costs and delay installation time. To solve these limitations, this study aimed to develop optimal butt fusion welding parameters of ECTFE applicable in the field. Due to the higher melting viscosity and lower surface activity of ECTFE, achieving reliable fusion joints requires precise control of hot plate temperature, fusion time, and welding pressure. In addition, in order to evaluate the comprehensive characterization and structural integrity of the welded joints produced under optimized parameters, tensile tests, FT-IR, SEM, density measurements, and automated visual inspection using an auto-scanner were performed. The experimental results demonstrated that the developed fusion joints satisfied the requirements specified in the DVS 2201 code standards. Multitask learning (MTL) refers to a machine learning approach in which one model is designed to handle several interconnected tasks at the same time. Instead of training separate models for each task, MTL shares representations among tasks, allowing them to leverage common patterns while maintaining task-specific distinctions. In this study, we compared two advanced machine learning techniques, namely multitask neural network (MTNN) and stacking ensemble learning, for predicting these parameters based on a shared dataset. A multitask neural network (MTNN) is a specific type of multitask learning (MTL) model that uses a deep neural network architecture to handle multiple related tasks simultaneously. In MTNN, different tasks share some hidden layers while having task-specific output layers. This shared representation allows the model to learn common patterns across tasks while maintaining task-specific outputs. Both methods are evaluated using RMSE and R<sup>2</sup> to determine their predictive accuracy and overall effectiveness.
