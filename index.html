Feasibility study of using magnetic and thermoelectric methods for the assessment of storage-induced changes in flux-cored welding wires利用磁性和热电方法评估自保护药芯焊丝储存引起变化的可行性研究

Aleksandra Świerczyńska, Balázs Varbai, Leszek Piotrowski

The lack of knowledge regarding whether a welding consumable remains suitable for the welding process or has undergone degradation that precludes its further use constitutes a significant challenge. Appropriate control procedures and effective evaluation methods may contribute to addressing this issue. This study presents the results of investigations concerning the applicability of magnetic and thermoelectric methods for such an assessment. Rutile flux-cored welding wires were stored in four different locations and subsequently collected for testing after 3, 6, and 12 months. Their suitability for industrial application was initially assessed on the basis of visual examination. Subsequently, measurements of thermoelectric power, characterization of bulk magnetic properties, and Magnetic Barkhausen Noise (MBN) were performed for each wire. Visual inspection confirmed the good surface condition of the wires despite the exceedance of the recommended limits of temperature and relative humidity during storage. The thermoelectric measurements showed that storage environment significantly affects the Seebeck Coefficient (SC) of welding wires, with marine conditions inducing rapid changes and less aggressive environments producing a gradual time dependent increase. Magnetic property measurements indicated that two of the evaluated parameters, namely the amplitude of MBN and the area under the envelopes of MBN, exhibited changes with storage time at each exposure site. Statistical analysis demonstrated that the parameters recorded during magnetic and thermoelectric measurements were characterized by a high degree of correlation, exceeding 0.9 in some cases. A strong linear relationship between the MBN Amplitude-to-Envelope Ratio and SC was observed for wires stored in the Urban Area and Workshop environments (r = 0.99 for both), indicating a close correlation between the evolution of their thermoelectric and magnetic properties during storage. Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Detonation gun ( D -gun) spraying is one of the most promising thermal spray technologies that would produce a high-quality ceramic coating. Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr coating is acceptable because of its superior mechanical characteristics and corrosion characteristic. The effectiveness of D -gun sprayed Cr₃C₂–NiCr coatings was evaluated under aggressive acidic conditions using 1 M H₂SO₄, which serves as a standardized laboratory electrolyte for assessing corrosion resistance relevant to industrial acidic environments, including the key process variables such as spraying distance, which is critically important in the determination of other coating properties such as porosity, adhesion, roughness, and hardness. Duplex stainless steel (DSS) 2205 has been sprayed with Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr at three spraying distances; 170 mm, 180 mm and 190 mm in an attempt to conduct the research on the behavior of Cr 3 C 2 -NiCr in acid conditions.
