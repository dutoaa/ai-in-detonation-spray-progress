Study on welding seam tracking for robotic arc welding under strong surface reflection强表面反光条件下机器人电弧焊焊缝跟踪研究

JS Gao, Kai Wang, Yuanyuan Qu, Yi Zheng, Hao Wang

Purpose This study aims to improve the environmental adaptability of teach-and-playback welding robots and the ability of laser vision sensing-based robotic seam tracking technology to cope with strong workpiece surface reflections and short lead distance between sensor and welding torch. Welding seam tracking algorithms have become a core research objective for promoting the stable application of robotic welding technology in complex welding scenarios. Design/methodology/approach This paper proposed an oblique-incidence laser vision sensing robotic welding seam tracking algorithm suitable for strong surface reflection conditions. First, to recover laser stripe feature from noisy images, a spatial coordinate attention module and dual-scale fusion module were developed to construct an image segmentation network. By fully leveraging contextual and multiscale feature information, this network enabled more precise restoration of laser stripes under large-area noise occlusion. Then, to further improve robustness, a self-iterative random sample consensus algorithm was developed to mitigate the effects of erroneous laser stripe inputs, facilitating accurate localization of welding seam position. Finally, a weld seam tracker was constructed. Findings The effectiveness of the proposed algorithm was validated on four workpieces with diverse surface conditions and laser stripe image data sets contaminated by large-area noise. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed tracker reduced the error in weld seam position pixel extraction accuracy by 81%, achieved a weld seam position pixel extraction accuracy of 0.52 pixels, and operated at an average processing speed of 37 FPS, under 22 mm lead distance. Practical implications The proposed approach could be applied to the robotic welding fabrication of products such as container girders, marine vessels, pressure vessels and vehicles. Physics-informed multi-sensor fusion framework for predicting weld penetration state and mechanistic discovery in CMT welding用于CMT焊接熔透状态预测与机理发现的物理信息多传感器融合框架

Yu Zhang, Kai Yang, Xudong Niu, Kailong Meng, Wenlong Zheng, Jiadui Chen

Accurate recognition of weld states is essential for ensuring structural integrity in cold metal transfer (CMT) welding. However, it remains challenging to develop models that are both robust to industrial disturbances and physically interpretable. In addition, the inherent opacity of current data-driven methods often limits their reliability in complex manufacturing environments. This study proposes ASPIS-Net, a robust physics-informed network for interpretable CMT weld-state monitoring. The proposed network adopts a dual-branch architecture with an adaptive gated fusion mechanism, which dynamically suppresses environmental noise while preserving critical acoustic and electrical features. To improve model transparency, a symbolic regression framework is further introduced to convert the learned representations into explicit physical equations, thereby aligning deep-learning predictions with the underlying welding thermodynamics. The proposed method was validated using an automated CMT welding platform, from which 5,000 synchronised acoustic and electrical signal samples were collected under four typical penetration states. The results show that ASPIS-Net achieves a classification accuracy of 99.36% and retains a Macro-F1 score of 75.59% under the 0 dB composite-noise condition. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量预测的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation. Aluminum Foam Sandwich (AFS) panels are extensively used in rail transportation, aerospace, and military applications because of their lightweight design, high specific strength and stiffness, and impact resistance. This study investigates the mechanical behavior of aluminum foam-to-aluminum sheet joints fabricated using Bobbin Tool Friction Stir Welding (BT-FSW). Based on the tested welding conditions, a traverse speed of 12 mm/min and a rotational speed of 1250 rpm were selected for joint fabrication. The mechanical properties of the welded panels were evaluated through compression, three-point bending, and interfacial shear tests. The welded sample exhibited a maximum compressive stress of 99.1 MPa at a compressive strain of 0.3135, a maximum load of 7250.8 N in three-point bending, and a maximum load of 2104 N in the interfacial shear test. Microstructure and surface performance of detonation gun sprayed 80Ni20Cr and NiCrAlY coatings on Al 6061-T6 substrate for structural applications面向结构应用的Al 6061-T6基体上爆炸喷涂80Ni20Cr和NiCrAlY涂层的微观组织与表面性能

T. Sampath kumar, Rajaram Ganesan, M.M. Reddy

Two protective coatings, 80Ni20Cr and NiCrAlY, were deposited on Al 6061-T6 alloy substrates using the Detonation Gun (D-Gun) thermal spray process. The deposited coatings were studied for their microstructure, phase composition, surface roughness, hardness, corrosion behaviour, wettability, and solid particle erosion resistance. Characterisation was done using SEM-EDS, XRD, AFM, nanoindentation, potentiodynamic polarisation, contact angle measurement, and erosion testing. SEM-EDS results showed that both coatings bonded well to the substrate with uniform thickness. The NiCrAlY coating had a denser microstructure with lower porosity compared to 80Ni20Cr. XRD identified CrNi phases with FCC crystal structure in 80Ni20Cr and Al₂NiY phases with orthorhombic structure in NiCrAlY. Nanocrystalline grain structure was observed in both coatings. AFM measurements showed that NiCrAlY had the lowest surface roughness among all three samples. Nanoindentation results showed that NiCrAlY hardness is approximately 10 times higher than 80Ni20Cr. Specifically, NiCrAlY showed a hardness of 7895.36 MPa (731.2 HV), compared with 780.11 MPa (72.25 HV) for 80Ni20Cr and 708.68 MPa for the uncoated Al 6061 substrate. Potentiodynamic polarisation tests in 3.5 wt% NaCl solution confirmed that NiCrAlY recorded the lowest corrosion current density of 6.625 × 10 -7 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.00567 mmpy. The 80Ni20Cr coating gave a corrosion current density of 1.585 × 10 - 6 A/cm² and a corrosion rate of 0.01427 mmpy. Both figures are considerably lower than the bare substrate at 0.5185 mmpy. Contact angle measurements confirmed superhydrophobic behaviour for NiCrAlY and near-superhydrophobic behaviour for 80Ni20Cr.
