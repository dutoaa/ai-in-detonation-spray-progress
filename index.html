Application and Adaptation of Arc-Based Welding Processes to Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing: A Review基于电弧焊工艺的应用与适应于电弧增材制造：综述

Ahmed Nabil Elalem, Husam Alrehaili, Mohammad Azad Alam, Sufian Raja, Azim Uddin

Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing (WAAM) has emerged as one of the most industrially scalable arc-based metal additive manufacturing (AM) technologies, using established Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW), Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW), and Plasma Arc Welding (PAW) processes to deposit metallic material layer-by-layer into near-net-shape components. This review presents a physics-based examination of the adaptation of traditional welding processes for AM applications, with emphasis on: (i) the thermodynamic and metallurgical principles governing melt pool formation, solidification, and solid-state transformations; (ii) the thermal and solidification features that WAAM shares with multi-pass welding and those that are distinctive to WAAM, with particular attention to the temperature gradient-to-solidification-rate ratio (G/R) and its role in controlling grain morphology; (iii) deposition path strategies and their influence on heat distribution and microstructural homogeneity; (iv) arc metal transfer modes and their thermal implications for AM; and (v) mechanisms of defect formation including porosity, solidification cracking, residual stress, and geometric deviation. Unlike general WAAM reviews, this article specifically positions conventional arc welding processes as the metallurgical and thermal foundation of WAAM and critically examines how their operating principles are transformed under repeated layer-by-layer deposition. Key governing equations linking process parameters to microstructure and properties are presented. Among GMAW variants, Cold Metal Transfer (CMT) provides low heat input, controlled droplet transfer, and low spatter, whereas GTAW and PAW allow wire feed and arc power to be set independently; the preferred process therefore depends on the alloy, required deposition rate, geometry, and surface quality. Hendrik Schulz, M. Stender

Abstract Resistance spot welding is a primary joining technique in the automotive and general metal manufacturing industries due to its cost-efficiency and potential for automation. As manufacturing transitions to Industry 4.0, an unprecedented volume of high-frequency process data is recorded. Recent advances rely on machine learning to correlate process data with weld quality. However, existing correlational models are typically trained under restricted laboratory conditions, lacking both physical interpretability and generalization across heterogeneous stack-up configurations with varying materials, coatings, thicknesses, and electrode guns. Here we show that by applying a two-phase causal discovery framework and time series clustering to 15,096 representative profiles derived from over 1.8 million welds, one can extract the structural dependencies of signal morphology from multicollinear stack-up configurations. Increased alloying content is associated with accelerated localized heating, advancing dynamic resistance peaks and suppressing secondary peaks in press-hardened steel. Sheet thickness proportionally scales dynamic resistance, displacement, and force amplitudes, while serving as a primary indicator for post-expansion stability. Growing interest in in-space servicing, manufacturing, and assembly has renewed attention on reliable metal joining for modular construction and on-orbit repair. Robotic welding is promising, but reduced-gravity and low-pressure operation remains poorly characterised and flight-like tests are limited. The UK Space Agency-supported ISPARK project is developing a physics-grounded digital twin for structured process de-risking. This paper evaluates a model-based prototype combining a finite-volume thermal weld-pool model, Gaussian-process (GP) and neural-network surrogates, and a simulated six-axis manipulator. The reduced model includes solid-liquid phase change, effective thermocapillary transport, and a local-pressure parameter. Gravity is assessed separately through a hydrostatic-to-capillary pressure ratio and does not enter the thermal energy equation. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于高密度聚乙烯摩擦搅拌焊焊接质量预测的计算与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution.
