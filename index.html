LiHao Yao, 张睿, Zixin Liang

Artificial intelligence (AI) has entered design courses, but its use often stops at tool operation. Taking Landscape Architectonics, an undergraduate course in landscape architecture programs, as the vehicle, this paper builds a teaching framework for generative design that integrates four elements: research findings, design precedents, AI-based recognition and judgment, and AI generation. The core claim is simple. Teaching students to “use AI” means teaching them to run an evidence-constrained generative design chain. “AI-driven” therefore carries two meanings: at the tool level, AI executes generation; at the teaching level, evidence steers generation, and the work of teaching lies in leading students from the former toward the latter. Among the four elements, research findings and design precedents are static knowledge that travels across sites. Research findings supply criteria for what counts as good. Design precedents supply examples of what to do, distilled from 13 street design guidelines into 114 retrievable situation packages. AI-based recognition and AI generation produce dynamic outputs anchored to a specific site. Students mobilize these elements through three stages—reading the site, grounding the context, and giving form—and a chain of evidence disciplines each stage. The framework follows three design principles: complementary elements, partial embedding, and explicit judgment. Three classroom examples show how the framework operates. R. Joselin, D. F. Jingle Jabha, Thirumurugan M.

In the present work, AA7075 aluminium alloy and AZ31 magnesium alloy were friction stir welded under three graphite interlayer conditions: 0, 1 and 2 g. All welding trials were conducted using a square-profile HCHCr tool at a rotational speed of 1000 rpm, traverse speed of 15 mm/min and axial force of 2 kN. This study is intended as a preliminary exploratory investigation of the influence of graphite quantity under one fixed processing condition rather than as a process-optimization study. The graphite-free and 2 g graphite conditions did not produce stable bonded regions. In contrast, the 1 g graphite condition generated a comparatively more continuous weld region, although substantial defects remained. FESEM examination of the 1 g condition revealed interfacial discontinuities, void-like regions, crack-like features, fragmented material zones and non-uniform flow structures, demonstrating incomplete consolidation. EDS analysis confirmed the simultaneous presence of Al, Mg and localized carbon-rich regions within the analysed weld zone, indicating partial incorporation of graphite but heterogeneous distribution. Accordingly, the results demonstrate an observed non-monotonic trend in weld formation under the investigated conditions, rather than establishing a universal quantitative relationship between graphite quantity and weld quality. Pressure-mediated intercrystalline welding of cellulose via a powder metallurgy-inspired approach for high-strength bulk materials借鉴粉末冶金方法实现纤维素的压力介导晶间焊接，制备高强度块体材料

Jing Yan, Maotao Cui, Likun Song, He Zhang, Xia Gao, Xiu Dong

Cellulose is an environmentally sustainable material with excellent mechanical properties; however, its widespread application is limited by processing constraints. Conventional solution-based processing involves complex procedures, poses environmental concerns, and is not suitable for fabricating bulk materials. Herein, we disclose a pressure-mediated intercrystalline welding phenomenon of cellulose at ambient temperature, which is further utilized to fabricate high-strength bulk cellulose materials via solvent-free consolidation. The pure cellulose bulk samples were fabricated by drawing lessons from the powder metallurgy techniques. Structural characterization combined with molecular dynamics simulations demonstrates that an appropriate molding pressure disrupts the network of hydrogen bonds of cellulose I and induces transformation into thermodynamically stable cellulose II through a transient amorphous intermediate. During this process, crystal-level welding was proved to occur synchronously with reconstruction of hydrogen bonds, generating continuous crystalline interfaces. Furthermore, high-speed mechanical pulverization was employed to induce in-situ fibrillation of the cellulose microfibrillar structure; by constructing a micro-nano interlocking network and creating significantly more crystalline welding sites, this approach further enhances interfacial bonding and the mechanical performance of the bulk materials. Meanwhile, we explore the influence of moisture content on the intercrystalline welding process, demonstrating that an optimal moisture content of 5% facilitates the rearrangement of hydrogen bonds. The optimized bulk sample (40 min fibrillation, 900 MPa molding pressure) exhibits a tensile strength of 72 MPa and a compressive strength of 667 MPa, exceeding those of many conventional plastics. Physics-based multiphysics modeling for process tailoring in ultrasonic welding of thermoplastic composites面向热塑性复合材料超声波焊接工艺调控的基于物理的多物理场建模

Qingxuan Wei, Rui Li, Jim Lua, Mark Walthers

This study presents a computationally efficient sequentially coupled thermo-mechanical modeling framework for predicting transient temperature evolution and enabling physics-based process tailoring in ultrasonic welding of thermoplastic composites. Unlike conventional one-way sequential or fully coupled approaches, the developed modeling strategy iteratively exchanges information between dynamic mechanical and transient heat-transfer analyses throughout the welding process, allowing the evolving temperature field to continuously update the mechanical response and associated heat generation. Viscoelastic and frictional heating are computed from the dynamic response of the composite adherends and energy director under ultrasonic excitation, using temperature-dependent thermal and mechanical properties. Temperature-dependent material properties were obtained from the literature, and a single hammering factor was calibrated using welding experiments at a vibration amplitude of 70 μm amplitude. Model predictions were validated against infrared thermography from experiments conducted at 80 μm and 90 μm amplitudes, showing close agreement in both magnitude and spatial/temporal trends of the temperature field, with prediction errors generally falling within the standard deviation of the experimental measurements. In addition, the simulated welded area closely matched the experimentally measured post-weld area, with molten interface fractions of 23% versus 29%, 87% versus 100%, and 94% versus 100% for the 70, 80, and 90 μm cases, respectively, demonstrating the model’s capability to establish quantitative relationships among process conditions, material behavior, temperature evolution, and weld quality. Finally, the validated model was used to determine an optimal welding time that improves weld quality and process efficiency. Implementing this predicted optimum in subsequent experiments resulted in reduced fiber distortion, a high welded fraction of 91.7%, compared to 92.3% predicted by simulation, and a 20% reduction in processing time, confirming the value of the proposed framework for efficient, physics-based digital process-tailoring of ultrasonic welding.本研究提出了一种计算高效的顺序耦合热力学建模框架，用于预测热塑性复合材料超声波焊接过程中的瞬态温度演化，并实现基于物理的工艺调控。与传统的单向顺序耦合或全耦合方法不同，所开发的建模策略在整个焊接过程中，在动态力学分析与瞬态传热分析之间迭代交换信息，使演化中的温度场能够持续更新力学响应及相应的产热。采用随温度变化的热学和力学性能，根据复合材料被焊件及导能筋在超声激励下的动态响应，计算黏弹性产热和摩擦产热。随温度变化的材料性能取自文献，并利用振动幅值为70 μm的焊接实验标定了一个锤击因子。通过振动幅值为80 μm和90 μm的实验所获得的红外热成像结果，对模型预测进行了验证。结果表明，预测温度场的数值大小及其空间和时间变化趋势均与实验高度一致，预测误差通常处于实验测量的标准差范围内。此外，模拟焊接面积与实验测得的焊后面积高度吻合：对于70、80和90 μm工况，模拟与实验的界面熔融面积占比分别为23%与29%、87%与100%、94%与100%。这表明该模型能够建立工艺条件、材料行为、温度演化与焊接质量之间的定量关系。最后，利用经验证的模型确定了能够改善焊接质量并提高工艺效率的最优焊接时间。在后续实验中采用这一预测最优值后，纤维畸变减小，焊接面积占比达到91.7%，而模拟预测值为92.3%，同时加工时间缩短了20%，证实了所提出框架对于高效、基于物理的超声波焊接数字化工艺调控的价值。

Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene用于预测高密度聚乙烯搅拌摩擦焊焊接质量的计算建模与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution.
