Their precision and efficiency make them ideal for high-volume production environments. The complex geometry of car body shells requires a wide variety of welding guns, a situation further complicated by different suppliers designing various sub-assemblies. The focus of this study is on the standardization and digital optimization of welding guns to enhance production efficiency. Thermal-sprayed WC–Co–Cr coatings are widely used to improve the wear and corrosion resistance of steel components operating under severe service conditions. This study investigates WC–Co–Cr coatings deposited by detonation spraying (DS), high-velocity oxy-fuel (HVOF), and atmospheric plasma spraying (APS) before and after ultrasonic nanocrystal surface modification (UNSM). The coatings were characterized using scanning electron microscopy and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM/EDS), together with microhardness, tribological, abrasive, erosive, impact-abrasive, cavitation, corrosion, oxidation, and valve endurance tests. The results demonstrate that the deposition technology strongly affects the coating structure and performance. UNSM produced a pronounced increase in microhardness, reaching 1468 HV for DS + UNSM, 1596 HV for HVOF + UNSM, and 1561 HV for APS + UNSM. Ultrasonic treatment also reduced material loss under abrasive, erosive, impact-abrasive, and cavitation conditions. APS + UNSM demonstrated the best performance in the principal laboratory wear tests, with the lowest abrasive mass loss (0.42–0.45 mg), erosive mass loss (0.55–0.60 mg), impact-abrasive mass loss (0.75–0.85 mg), and cavitation mass-loss rate (0.33–0.38 mg/h). In tribological testing, APS + UNSM exhibited the lowest coefficient of friction (0.28), compared with 0.32 for HVOF + UNSM and 0.36 for DS + UNSM. In valve endurance testing, HVOF + UNSM showed the lowest plunger displacement growth rate (0.050 mm/h) and the longest calculated time to reach the 2 mm displacement criterion (22 h). Ignatius Echezona Ekengwu, Bonaventure Onyeka Ekengwu, Ikechukwu Okoli, Obiora Clement Okafor

Weld groove geometry and post-weld cooling regime are among the least systematically investigated factors influencing the performance of arc-welded joints, and even when they have been studied, the analysis has almost always been limited to a single optimisation criterion. This paper re-examines shielded metal arc welding of AISI/SAE 1020 mild steel across a 55–60° single-V groove range and three quenching media (air, water, palm oil), using six mechanical responses, and advances the analysis in four directions. First, a normalised Type III effect-share analysis establishes the practical rather than merely statistical weight of each factor: the quenching medium accounts for 70.3–94.6% of the estimated effect across the six responses, while groove angle accounts for 4.4–25.7%, and the factor interaction never exceeds 3.2%. Second, the tensile records are reconciled against the reported applied loads, recovering the specimen cross-sections actually used and permitting two derived responses that the original analysis did not report: the modulus of toughness, which orders the media 8.68, 5.96 and 3.66 MJ m⁻³ for air, water and palm oil, and the Hollomon strain-hardening exponent, obtained on true stress and true plastic strain axes, which falls from 0.528 for air-cooled weldments to 0.292 and 0.253 for water- and oil-quenched material. Third, four independent multi-criteria schemes, namely Shannon-entropy-weighted grey relational analysis, equal-weight grey relational analysis, the technique for order of preference by similarity to ideal solution, and Derringer–Suich desirability, are applied to the same candidate set, and all four return the same ordering. Fourth, machine-learning surrogates of the recovered stress–strain records locate where the medium acts: over the strain interval common to all three media a functional analysis of variance attributes only 1.16% of the stress variance to the medium, and truncating the comparison at that strain reverses the ordering, so the advantage of air cooling is one of extensibility rather than strength. Gaussian-process sampling returns credible intervals near ±9% on the toughness values and preserves the ranking in every draw. Computational and machine learning modelling approaches for weld quality predictions in friction stir welding of high-density polyethylene高密度聚乙烯摩擦搅拌焊焊缝质量预测的计算与机器学习建模方法

A. Alhourani, I. Barsoum, J. Sheikh-Ahmad, S. Deveci

Friction stir welding (FSW) has gained recognition as an effective technique for joining thermoplastic materials. This study presents a comprehensive investigation to predict FSW characteristics such as weld temperatures, vertical axial forces, material flow and defect formation in FSW of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) using both computational and machine learning approaches, validated by experimental results. A three-dimensional Coupled Eulerian-Lagrangian (CEL) numerical model is developed to simulate the HDPE welding process under varying process parameters of rotational and welding speeds, and bottom preheating conditions. The CEL numerical model is validated against experimental data, showing markedly good agreement in predicting void formation, thermal and mechanical responses. Results indicate that preheating the bottom surface proved effective in reducing thermal gradients and stabilizing vertical force fluctuations. Quantitatively, bottom-surface preheating at 60 °C reduced peak plunge force from 1600 N close to 1100 N and raised peak temperatures from 70 °C (without preheat) to about 100 °C, hence smoothing through-thickness gradients and stabilizing the axial force distribution. In parallel, two multilayer perceptron artificial neural networks (ANNs) are trained on experimental data to predict temporal temperature and axial force responses with R2 between 0.91 and 0.99, demonstrating high accuracy and strong generalization across the tested FSW parameter space. The computational CEL simulations accurately predicted weld zone morphology, including stir zone, thermo-mechanically affected zone and heat-affected zone, however the combined CEL–ANN framework enables rapid generation of process maps that highlights defective and defect-free welding windows, with optimal conditions obtained at 1500 rpm and 30 mm/min with 60 °C preheating, and poor weldability observed at 1000 rpm without preheating due to wormhole formation.
