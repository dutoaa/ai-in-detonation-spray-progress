## Open Questions

- What detonation-specific dataset would adequately connect gas composition, charge conditions, firing frequency, powder characteristics, stand-off distance, and substrate thermal history to particle state and coating performance?
- Which sensors can reliably resolve individual detonation cycles and particle or substrate responses, and which signals predict subsequent porosity, adhesion, erosion, or corrosion rather than merely correlate with operating settings?
- Can physics-informed representations generalize across coating chemistries, substrates, powder batches, and D-Gun systems better than models trained only on nominal machine parameters?
- How should small-data coating models quantify uncertainty and be evaluated using independent batches, machines, or prospective experiments while avoiding leakage during tuning?
- How can closed-loop optimization balance conflicting performance targets while enforcing equipment safety, substrate temperature limits, and reliable coating acceptance criteria?

## Outlook

The most defensible next step is a detonation-specific experimental and modeling program rather than direct adoption of welding-model results. A practical starting point would pair controlled D-Gun trials with synchronized process measurements and standardized coating characterization, then compare transparent statistical baselines with tree ensembles and physics-informed surrogates. Evaluation should reserve independent deposition runs or material batches and report uncertainty alongside predictive accuracy. Multi-objective, human-supervised experiment selection could then target improvements in porosity, adhesion, wear, and corrosion before any move toward closed-loop control. The October metadata supplies useful methods and physical context for this progression, but not evidence that autonomous AI optimization of detonation spray coating has already been achieved.
