While several papers were broader welding studies, the directly relevant detonation-spray items highlighted two important trends: using AI/ML to model process–property relationships in spray deposition, and combining detonation-sprayed coatings with post-treatment or hierarchical design to push hardness, wear resistance, and corrosion resistance higher. ## Field Progress

- AI is becoming a practical tool for coating R&D rather than only an academic add-on, especially for predicting performance from process variables and microstructure features.
- Detonation-sprayed coatings are being engineered in more sophisticated forms, including gradient structures and post-treated surfaces, to improve corrosion and wear resistance.
- Quantitative microscopy is moving toward automated defect segmentation, which can reduce manual image analysis bottlenecks in spray-coating characterization.
- The evidence base is expanding from single-property reporting to multi-property evaluation, including hardness, adhesion, scratch response, wear, and corrosion in the same study.
- Comparative studies across APS, HVOF, HVAF, and detonation spraying continue to clarify where detonation spraying excels, especially in dense hard coatings.
- Process parameter sensitivity remains central, with spray distance, gas ratio, and post-treatment load emerging as especially influential variables.

## Open Questions

- Which machine-learning architectures best generalize across different detonation spray systems, powders, and substrates?
- Can high-quality detonation coating performance be predicted reliably from limited experimental datasets, or are larger multimodal datasets required?
- How should process parameters and post-treatments be jointly optimized to balance hardness, adhesion, roughness, and corrosion resistance?
- What is the best way to integrate SEM-image segmentation with process data so that microstructure defects can be traced back to spray conditions?
- How transferable are models developed for cold spray or APS to detonation spray coating diagnostics and optimization?
- Can gradient detonation coatings be designed algorithmically for target environments rather than through trial-and-error powder selection?

## Outlook

The outlook for AI in detonation spray and detonation spray coating is positive and increasingly concrete. The most promising direction is a closed-loop workflow that combines process simulation, experimental coating synthesis, automated microstructure analysis, and predictive models for wear and corrosion performance. In the near term, AI is likely to have the largest impact in parameter optimization, defect detection, and data-efficient screening of coating compositions and post-treatments. As datasets grow and multimodal methods mature, detonation spray research should move toward faster, more reproducible coating design with better performance targeting and less reliance on manual iteration.
